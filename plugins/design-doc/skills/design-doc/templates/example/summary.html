tinyq replaces the jobs table every worker polls with one durable append-only log per tenant. A job is safe as soon as the log acks it, and a worker already parked on a long poll gets the next job pushed to it, so the wait between "enqueued" and "running" stops being a timer and becomes the work itself.

Getting work to a worker
Every worker wakes on a five-second timer and asks the table for work.
The dispatcher pushes each job down a connection a worker is already holding.
Dispatch latency stops tracking the poll interval (DQ4).
Where a job lives
One shared table in the application database, alongside everything else it stores.
One append-only log per tenant, in its own SQLite file, acked after the batch's WAL flush.
A job survives the loss of any single machine, and no tenant waits on another tenant's flush (DQ1, A2).
Two workers, one job
Two polls can race for the same row, and nothing durable records who won.
A worker holds a lease it renews; an expired lease re-queues the job, and producers set an idempotency key.
Every failure mode collapses into one story, at the price of a duplicate the consumer can detect (DQ2, A1).
Finished work
Completed rows stay in the table until somebody prunes them, and the scans get slower.
Completed jobs move to object storage after 24 hours; a restore replays the archive plus the live tail.
The replay rate is still an estimate until the restore spike runs (V2).

Why this shape

The log is the only durable state in the system. Dispatcher memory, queue positions and worker leases are all derived from it, so recovery is one procedure — reopen the file, replay unexpired leases, resume — instead of one per component. That is the whole argument, and every other choice here follows from keeping it true.

What we are not changing

Producers still enqueue over HTTP and still choose their own idempotency keys. Payloads stay small, with anything over 64 KB going to object storage behind a pointer (A4). Multi-region durability stays out of scope, and scheduled jobs are recorded as an open question rather than designed here (Q2).

Where we want pushback

The load-bearing claim is that every consumer can absorb a duplicate delivery (A1). We believe it, we have not confirmed it in writing, and if one consumer cannot, this becomes an exactly-once design with a dedup ledger in the hot path and most of its simplicity gone. The second thing worth arguing about is whether lease renewals belong on the dispatch connection or an endpoint of their own; we left that open on purpose (DQ4, V3).
