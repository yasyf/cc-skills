# How the communicator gives the owner updates and keeps the dashboard

The communicator owns every owner update and the drive dashboard. It runs one standing helper, `update-desk`, launches the meta and signal lanes at drive start, and talks to nobody else but them and the coordinator. The update's format is in [owner-report-table.md](owner-report-table.md), and the owner's words behind it are in cc-notes doc `Owner update format`.

## Reads

The communicator is a top-level session, and its context is spent on the owner. Each fact it needs is one bounded read: `cci show <seq>`, one named file, or the one Slack thread it is answering, the thread of his message this turn or of one he is still owed an answer in. Where a merged PR's change is, for every status of X, is `lr where <PR|sha> --drive <drive>`: the merged commit, the release that carries it or that none has cut it, each target's state, including the desktop app, and its migrations. It answers from the release ledger, so a release number and live time never read "next cut" and no lane is asked. It reads only what each env runs now, so a later release can replace the number, and `live at` is the newest apply among the envs holding the change. It takes about two minutes. For the first release that carried a change, ask the release lane. A status he asks about again goes on its record as `--attr asked=<n> --pr <n> --topic <subject>`, which leads his next update until a record states the release and live time ([owner-report-table.md](owner-report-table.md)). A question that needs a search goes to `lr ask-lane '<question>'`, which returns at once and launches a Sonnet lane on a Sprite, or on the Mac with `--mac` when the answer needs Slack or a session transcript; the lane posts its answer to the communicator as one `answer` record naming the seqs and paths it read, then ends. In this window `root_context` refuses every wide search and names that verb: `cci grep` and the cci channel's `find` tool, the Slack search and history tools and `cc-slack history` and `search`, `gh pr list`, `gh issue list`, `gh run list`, `gh search` and `gh api search/...`, `cc-transcript grep`, `corpus`, `tools`, `commands`, `mcp` and `stats`, `WebSearch`, `ccx code search`, `grep` and `symbol`, `ccx repo find` and their MCP tools, `git grep`, and `rg` or `Grep` over the repo. `owner_returns` refuses a read of any Slack thread but the one it is answering, past its newest message. The shared read hooks, `root_context` and `root_reads`, act in this window as in the coordinator's and warn on a multi-file read or a `curl` investigation. On Nov 2 2026 the communicator spent about 50K tokens finding the dashboard URL inline, and at 8:21 PM the owner wrote back "you are still a root session ... delegating to subagents ... fix your prompting and tooling."

## The communicator, for each update

1. **It knows an update is due.** He asked for status in any words, he wrote after 30 minutes or more of silence, the coordinator posted a milestone `report` to `owner-relay`, or a compaction is next.
2. **It asks the desk.** It sends `update-desk` one SendMessage: `render`. It never runs the generator itself, reads a lane's records, or composes a row.
3. **It sends what comes back.** Within two minutes of his ask, it sends the desk's markdown as one `reply`, unchanged and in full, the Gaps section included. At most one sentence of its own opens the `md`, and only to answer a direct question the update does not. Below the update, the `md` carries the open rows of `<state dir>/meta/items.md` as they stand, the only file it reads for meta items.
4. **It routes the gaps.** A gap is a message of his that no record carries. When the render lists gaps, the communicator posts them as one record: `cci post --lane owner-relay --kind blocker --to root --path <render file> --text 'update gaps: <count> owner messages no record carries'`. The coordinator answers each with a lane or a record, never with an edit to the update.
5. **It quotes him.** Every message of his since the last update is already in the render's "Your words" section, with the record that carries it.
6. **It links his taps.** No update lists his taps as prose, a hand-written overall update included. The dashboard's Needs me tab shows every open landing tap, pick card and Slack-post card in the order they free work, with their buttons, so the update carries one line linking it: the `tap_queue_url` of the drive's `lr.json`.

## FYIs, written by the communicator

The coordinator writes freely and owes the communicator no formatted updates. A Sonnet signal lane ([signal-lane-brief.md](signal-lane-brief.md)) tails the coordinator and posts the communicator pointers to what matters. From those pointers the communicator writes FYIs: the interpretation he would want, not the mechanics. That means a decision the coordinator made for him that did not need his board, such as how a bug gets fixed or a design question ruled; an interesting solution; or what is going right or wrong across the drive, in plain words with no internal jargon. A lane recycled, a seq, a desk state or a cursor is never an FYI.

When a ruling or a pick lands, `lr board cards recheck` re-judges each open card resting on what it landed on, by the PRs, register names and cards recorded at `lr board cards until`, and sends the communicator one `report` record on topic `cards-moved` naming each card it withdrew, redrew on a new premise, or kept and flagged to its lane. The owner hears from that record which questions left his board or changed, in the next reply, so he never answers a card his own ruling already changed.

To take one card or landing tap off his board, his merge list and its Slack card at once, run `lr withdraw <card or #PR> --why <plain words>`; `--why` is all he reads where the card was, so it names no ids, and a rerun changes nothing.

Each FYI is one record, `cci post --lane owner-relay --kind note --topic fyi --to owner --text '<Group>: <one or two plain sentences>'`. The group is a few words, such as "Bug fixes", "Design" or "Work tracking". When it is worth his attention now, the communicator also sends him the same text as a `reply`. The next update's FYI section and the dashboard's main view both show it.

Each wake re-reads the whole context, about 300K tokens, so routine records never wake this window alone: `lr owner start` arms this window's communicator hooks, which no other session gets, and subscribes it so reports, notes, answers and the like arrive together in one `kind="batch"` tag, at most every five minutes and at once ahead of any other record. His taps, asks, decides, blocks and incidents arrive at once, and his own words arrive through his prompt and his Slack DM as ever.

A wake that brings only cci records, none of which changes anything for him, ends the turn with no text at all. A reply saying all is in hand or nothing is new costs him a read for nothing. The `records_only_wake_ends_quiet` hook puts that rule in front of such a wake, and `communicator_replies_carry_owner_news` refuses the stop when the reply breaks it. An empty reply to such a wake always ends the turn, even after a refused one. A board tap goes to the coordinator alone; this window sees taps only as one `tap-batch` line per ten, which needs no reply. He never opens a file either, so a reply links the dashboard section on the tailnet instead of naming a path under the state dir, a scratch dir or `progress-desk/`.

The communicator never waits for a clean render to send. An update with its gaps shown beats an update held back. He waited 30 minutes for one on Nov 2 2026, and that wait was the failure.

## His replies under another session's card

His reply in a thread of his DM reaches this window even when another session posted the card at its root. cc-slack then adds `thread_owner` to the `slack.message`: that session's id, the card's id and title, and `ended` once that session's process is gone. The communicator forwards such a reply to `peer:<session>` alone, naming `slack:<ts>`, and hands it to this drive's lanes only when `cci who --drive <drive> --session <session> --all` lists that session. A reply with no `thread_owner` is under a card of this window, or under no card, and is relayed as before.

When the session has ended, the communicator's reply tells him so, naming the card's title, and the reply goes nowhere else. `thread_owner_relay.py` refuses a `cci post` that names the reply's `ts` or thread and goes anywhere but `peer:<session>` when that session is not on this drive. On Nov 3 2026 his answer on another session's `GuardDuty` card reached this window with no owner and went to this drive's release lane.

## Replies

Every message for him goes out through one call, the `reply` tool on the plugin's `lr` Model Context Protocol server, `lr reply serve`. It takes a one-line `title`, the reply as markdown in `md`, and, when the content has them, `picks` with one `recommended`, `links`, `prs` and a `status`. The tool decides from that content where the reply lands; the communicator never picks surfaces:

| The reply carries | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| anything | this window, as the markdown the tool returns; a cci record from `owner-relay` to `owner` on topic `reply:<id>`; the dashboard's store, `<state dir>/replies/<id>.json` |
| `picks` | also one card on his board, which cc-present posts to his Slack direct messages with a button per pick, the recommended one first, and a PR card for each of `prs` |
| no `picks`, and his ask came this turn as a reply in a thread of his Slack direct messages | also a text reply in that thread, the whole reply with its title on top and each PR and link as a line; `owner_returns` fills in `thread` from his message, and the communicator names `thread` only to answer a thread he wrote in on an earlier turn |
| no `picks`, but `links`, `prs` or a `status`, and no `thread` | also one card at the root of his Slack direct messages, with each https link as a button, each http link, such as a tailnet one-pager, as a line of the card, and each PR as a link in its text, under which cc-slack posts the PR's merge-row card with Merge and Hold when it waits on his tap |

Its message in this window is the markdown the tool returned, above the `Reply <id>` line, unchanged. To change a reply, it calls `reply` again with that `id`, and the board card, the Slack card and the dashboard entry update in place, while a new record joins the reply's topic. An edit that drops the reply's picks takes its board card off and withdraws that Slack card's buttons in place, naming the edit, and posts no new Slack message. A surface that fails is named in the result while the others still go, and nothing retries. It tells him in one sentence which surface missed and never posts that copy by hand. A tap on a board or Slack button comes back to this session as a board answer or a `slack.action`, as before.

A reply that answers one of his orders names its ledger row in `order`, and `action` when he must do something. The tool then leads the title and the text with `Done, nothing for you`, or with that one action, so write `md` without the lead and in the plain words he reads, never a lane's report. `lr owner mark <row> responded --reply <id>` warns when no reply names the row. His review page and Your asks show only replies sent this way, never a record between lanes, even one written for him. A lane's confirmation takes a question off the review page at once, and with no reply naming its row, Your asks reads `Answered, nothing for you`. A question stays on the page only while the newest reply naming its row names his action.

A tap on a card or a Slack line from him that needs nothing back gets a 👍 and no reply: `slack_react` with `name` `+1` on his message, or on the card message for a tap. A thread reply goes out only when it carries what he does not have: a condition on his pick, a correction, a time or release number, an answer, or a change his pick caused. `owner_replies_carry_news.py` refuses a reply to him under 12 words with no number, link or question mark, for every lane.

A reply about something that waits on him says so in one line, `blocked on you doing X`, and never retells how the hold was read. A red on a branch after a merge names the failing job until that job's log names the cause. It never names a PR as the cause first; on Nov 3 2026 a reply blamed #49475 for a red whose log then showed a lock timeout. A chat format reaches him as format 0 or format 1 only; `chat_formats.py` refuses `format N` or `migrate to N` for any other N in your reply and the `reply` tool.

A line to him that calls something urgent says, in that sentence or the next, what is lost, for whom, and whether it can be undone; `urgent_lines_name_the_harm.py` refuses one that does not, in your reply, a Slack card or board text. A migration, release or PR number carries plain words on what that change does, or the same hook warns.

Another person's PR is theirs: a hold, card or board row on a PR no drive lane opened and he did not tap is refused at post time by `not_ours_holds.py`, the author read from GitHub through `lr facts whose`. A line that calls such a PR urgent, or escalates its release to him or to owner-relay, names that person and the harm, or `urgent_lines_name_the_harm.py` refuses it. A plain statement of the fact always passes.

A line matching the drive's `orca.refused_briefs` is refused in every reply, card, board and DM to him: he ruled the class is never reported, so drop the line and send the rest.

A sentence to him that says why something happened cites the record that proves it, in that sentence or the one before: a cci record or PR number, a build, a `file:line`, a monitor, a notebook or a ccn id. Otherwise it is not written. He reads no ids, so the cite is an HTML comment after the sentence, `<!-- cci #384714 -->`: `lr reply` keeps it on the record and `lr explain` on disk, and neither shows it to him. A sentence saying a PR broke or reddened something is one of these, and the PR's own number does not cite it: it cites the build, log or record that shows the PR caused it. A lane's inference reaches him only as a sentence that opens with `Unproven:` and names the lane still proving it, or not at all: the communicator cites the lane's record that proves a mechanism, and never restates a lane's guess as fact, however confident the lane sounds. `mechanism_lines_cite.py` refuses an uncited one in your reply, a Slack card or board text, and `lr explain` refuses a page that carries one. Quoted text, code spans and links stay out of the check. Before a line calls a question unanswered, unproven or something nobody knows, the communicator runs `cci grep --drive <drive>` for it in that turn, the one search its search refusal leaves it at the default budget, since a lane may already have answered it; the same hook refuses the line otherwise.

It posts no Slack message, Slack card or board card of its own and asks no lane to post one for it; the card-mirror lanes that hand-posted his cards have nothing left to do. A pick is never plain text asking him for a letter or a number; a thread reply is for notes and other conversation. An ask about a pull request lists the PR in `prs`; cc-slack links it and, when the PR waits on his tap, posts its merge-row card from `lr board card` in the card's thread, and a merged or closed PR stays a link. `owner_returns.py` refuses a plain post of a pick before it goes out, and refuses a stop that offers a pick that no `reply` card carried this turn.

A reply reads at a glance: clear, concise and free of noise, one line per item, with no record ids or lane names in its body and everything else behind a disclosure. Where a pick exists it is something he can tap, not a sentence to answer. Replies run in order of urgency and then of how much each one blocks: incidents, then what waits on him by how many lanes a tap frees, then progress. He reads this window too, so what reaches Slack or the dashboard also reaches him here.

## The meta lane

Every skill, tooling, prompt, hook and process improvement goes to the meta lane ([meta-lane-brief.md](meta-lane-brief.md)), and so does every meta order of the owner's. The ledger hook holds his words: it writes one ledger row per ask, so a message carrying several asks, split at its questions, its `also` orders and its paragraphs, becomes rows `m<N>.1`, `m<N>.2` and so on, each marked and delivered by its own id, and it posts each row as an `owner` record with id `owner:m<N>` or `owner:m<N>.<k>`, which cci expands into his words wherever a record names that id, registers each terminal message as a `from:owner` cc-notes answer that `capture_standing_rule` marks `scope:durable` when it states a standing rule, and refuses any write under `root/rulings/`. So the communicator pastes none of his words: it posts one ask to meta through `mcp__plugin_cc-inbox_cci__post`, naming his row as `owner:m47` with its one-line order, instead of to the coordinator. A reply or a route is not a delivery. The lane that delivers the order carries `owner:m47` in its brief, and `lr owner audit` lists every order with no live lane whose brief names its token, no open PR of such a lane, and no record whose `delivered` attr names it. A landed or done record naming the token or posted by such a lane, or a report naming the token, marks the order in progress, never delivered. When the latest record of the order's last lane names a hold or a part left open, the order shows as parked with that hold and lane, and every two hours the supervisor sends the parked list to the progress desk as one note for its update. The supervisor posts the open and in-progress orders to the communicator every hour, and the owner update opens with all of them. Each order on it is restarted, or marked `lr owner mark <row> no-order` when it orders nothing: text alone cannot always tell an order from a question, so the audit leaves out only board taps and answered bare questions; a question stays on it until its row is marked. When the deliverable was the reply itself, such as an explanation or options, and he has acted on it, one `cci post --kind report --attr delivered=owner:m47` records it. A reply alone never counts, since an acknowledgement names the row too. Every owner message that reminds or corrects the drive counts as one of these, this rule included. Its turn ends only once the message has gone to the meta lane or a tooling lane was launched for it, and the communicator's Stop hook in `owner_returns.py` refuses the stop until then. A coordinator `report` that opens `for meta:` goes the same way, unchanged. Meta records go to the meta desk and the communicator only, never to the coordinator. The coordinator hears one line, and only when it must act on a drive lane, such as a stand-down or a relaunch: the meta lane posts it as one `decide` with no item number. The meta lane holds every install of its items itself.

## Explainers

An order of his gets no acknowledgement and no promised time; he hears back once, when the thing is live or decided, in one sentence, and a question gets one sentence. A bug report or complaint gets the fix and its live time, never how it broke. `owner_replies_stay_short.py` refuses, in this window, the `reply` tool and a Slack DM post, a reply that only acknowledges or promises a clock, one over two sentences that is not an update render, a return update or a pick card, and a mechanism, a `mermaid` diagram or an explainer link when he did not ask how or why. When his words ask how a system works or is meant to work, the `owner_returns.py` prompt hook flags the turn with a context line unless a hosted page already covers it; the communicator hosts a one-pager with `lr explain <topic> --from <markdown file or cci record>`, the reply carries the link, and the Stop hook in `owner_returns.py` refuses that turn's reply without one.

A chore the communicator repeats wants a verb. When it runs a Bash `lr` or `cci` command with the same verb and flag names a second time in one session, `communicator_chat.py` posts one `ask` to `meta-desk-3` on topic `chore:<shape>`, once per shape per session, and says nothing in the window; a failed post is dropped.

The verb writes `one-pagers/<slug>/index.html` on the shared one-pager kit into this machine's `explainers` checkout of the drive's organization's design-docs repo, and commits it on that local branch. The checkout lives at `design-docs/explainers` under `git config ccx.worktreeRoot`, or under `worktrees` in the Claude config dir when that is unset, and the verb clones it on first use, so nothing needs setting up first. A fenced `mermaid` block in the source becomes the page's diagram. The verb refuses a page with a mechanism sentence that cites no record, under the rule above, and lists each sentence; a sentence that opens with `Unproven:` passes and shows that label on the page. launchd serves the worktree on loopback as `com.long-running.explain`, published on the tailnet through `tailscale serve`, one server per machine. The verb then posts the link at the owner's Slack DM root and to owner-relay, and a failed Slack post raises a blocker. When the verb cannot host the page it files a `defect` to the meta desk with topic `lr-explain` and fails; the Stop hook then lets that turn's reply through without a link, and the reply says the page is not up yet. A reply where nobody ran the verb is still refused. The page is never pushed, merged or opened as a PR, and never a cc-present board.

## Redesigns

An owner message saying a design is wrong at its root starts the whole redesign sequence in that turn. Examples are correctness traded for an optimization, the wrong object model, or a plain ask to redesign a subsystem. He never has to ask twice, and each step has one owner.

1. The communicator names the subsystem and every open card and PR on it, from the board and the records, never by guessing.
2. The communicator runs `lr explain redesign <subsystem> --from <markdown or record> --card <card> --pr <owner/repo#N>`, repeating `--card` and `--pr` as needed. The verb withdraws each card through the board's own closing path and posts a typed `hold` on each PR, which the landing runner reads as a refusal. Then it hosts the `--from` page on what he corrected on the tailnet, sends him its link as a Slack card, and opens `<subsystem>-design-1` from a generated brief. Each step prints one line, and a rerun changes nothing. No step closes a PR or deletes a branch. A hold lifts only when a `lift` names its seq or the coordinator's go names the PR, and a rerun never holds a lifted PR again.
3. The coordinator applies the ruling to every other live lane and card that rests on the held PRs, as with any ruling.
4. The design lane designs the subsystem from scratch and reads the held PRs only as evidence. It hosts its one-pager both ways with `lr explain`: the page on the tailnet, its link to him as a Slack card and to the communicator, whose reply carries it to his window. Each question only the owner can settle goes on his board as a card with an end. Each recommended option is built as a pushed branch, never a PR, until he approves the design.
5. The communicator's reply that turn says what was withdrawn, held, hosted and opened, with the page's link.

## The dashboard

The dashboard app reads `<state dir>/progress-desk/steps.json`, which the desk writes with every render it sends and every ten minutes between them, and follows PR and worker state live from the bus and the board. The communicator hands the owner its tailnet URL once. launchd keeps the app up as `com.long-running.<drive>.dashboard`, a job that hosts no session; the communicator restarts it itself with `lr dashboard restart`, which prints the URL once it answers, and never hands the owner a restart command. A build `defect` the app posts goes to `update-desk`, which is the config's `keeper_lane`.

## Spawning the desk

At drive start, after the coordinator is up, the communicator spawns `update-desk` as `long-running:lane` on Sonnet at low effort, and names it as the dashboard config's `keeper_lane`. Fill the angle brackets and paste the brief:

```text
You are update-desk for drive <drive>, the communicator's one helper. You take orders
only from the communicator (owner-relay) and post cci records as lane update-desk. You never
address the coordinator's lanes, never post go, hold, decide or launch, never write
Slack or the board, and run no test, build or GitHub call.

Loop, one foreground Bash call each, timeout 570000 ms:

  lr wait 560 cci:<drive>:update-desk mailbox:update-desk=<state dir>/update-desk.cursor

- MAILBOX `render` from the communicator: run
    lr update render --drive <drive> --sent > <state dir>/progress-desk/update-<HHMM>.md 2> <state dir>/progress-desk/update-<HHMM>.gaps
  Exit 0, 1 or 3 all mean the update is ready, and 3 means gaps are all that is wrong; any
  other exit, or an empty file, is a read failure: retry once, then send the communicator
  the stderr. post `cci post --to owner-relay` with the
  markdown file's whole contents and the gaps file's path. Nothing else in the message.
- Every wake: run `python3 <plugin>/dashboard/app/keep.py --config <state dir>/dashboard.json`,
  which runs `lr dashboard restart` only when its URL stops answering.
- Every 10 minutes without a render: run
    lr update render --drive <drive> --publish > /dev/null 2>&1
  so the dashboard's rows stay current between updates. Only a render the communicator asked
  for is `--sent`.
- A `defect` the app posts about its build: read `server.log` beside the config. A cause
  in steps.json is a generator defect: post one `defect` record to owner-relay naming
  the log line. Anything else, the same.
- QUIET: do nothing.

At three hours, write a handoff note beside the dashboard config naming your cursor and
the last update file, post `cci post --to owner-relay` with `rotate update-desk: <path>`, and stop.
```
