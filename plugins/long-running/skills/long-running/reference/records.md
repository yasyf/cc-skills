# Between the sessions

- A session posts its own `owner`, `answer`, `handoff` or `defect`, and a handler his `owner` or `answer` and a `handoff`, with the drive's `record` tool, which picks its lane and addresses the session it goes to, or the lane its `to` names, refusing a lane no session of the drive reads. An `answer` naming `waits` goes to the session holding that job's order, the meta session for a drive tooling order, else the coordinator session hearing it. A job's question in the drive decides its card: once an `owner` or `answer` record of his words on an open question goes out, named by its `waits` id, the ask seq in `re`, `thread_card`, the `topic` `card:<thread_ts>` or his permalink under its card in whatever thread the record names, the tool ends that question in the same call, answered for his answer and withdrawn for his owner words about that card's work, and the card follows through one `card-answer` call, showing his words with no buttons; his feedback on the drive under a product card leaves it asking. That ends only the card his words sit under, so a record naming a `ruling` with its `url`, his owner ruling on his work included, also keeps that ruling as his drive answer in the same call and prints its listing: each other card asking him, undelivered part of an open order holding more than one and PR not yet live it could change, which the watch's `ANSWER` line chases until `drive answer <id>` marks each, `--dropped` for each his words remove. His answer also ends, as superseded, each older open question of the same job it decides, as Jev reads it, and his tap on a card ends its question through cc-slack's card command, which cc-slack reruns until the drive takes it, the card showing the tap recorded only then. A PR card stays up: only the holding session's `--rework <PR>` on his order or on the job carrying it withdraws it. It refuses an `answer` naming only questions that ended, since his answer there is recorded already. `record_lines` refuses such a record on the cci `post` tool and `landing_guard` one by `cci post` in Bash, naming the tool. Every other record, and any other from an agent, goes through cci's `post` tool, whose `record_lines` hook checks it and `cci post` in Bash skips. Every session and agent posts, reads and closes records with the cci MCP tools (`post`, `read`, `close`) whenever its cci server is connected, and `landing_guard` refuses `cci post` in Bash while it is, by his words "metadata session is using the CLI insrqad of mcp for `cci post`"; `cci post` in Bash is for a session whose cci server is gone. While cci is down, or the receiving session's cci server dropped so it hears no channel record, a session reaches another with ListAgents, then SendMessage to `<drive>-<role>`, such as `<drive>-meta`, and posts the record once cci is back; the `record` tool's refusal names that route. A disconnect is never a reason a message did not go, by his words "use regualr claude code sesion messaging until it sback then there sno excuse".
- On cci, name the writer with `--lane communicator`, `--lane root` or `--lane meta`, since cci's default is never `root`, and address `root@<drive>`, `communicator@<drive>` or `meta@<drive>`, since a bare lane no session here reads can land on another drive. The meta session reads `meta@<drive>` at cursor `meta`.
- A sub-coordinator, the coordinator session for one project, writes as `--lane <lane>` and is addressed at `<lane>@<drive>`, `<lane>` being the project's name lowercased with each run of other characters a dash (`Iris` is `iris`, `Sand SQL` is `sand-sql`). Its block names the lane, it reads at cursor `coordinator-<lane>`, and its project's watch lines reach it there.
- The drive routes an `owner`, `answer`, `handoff`, `posted` or `refused` record from the communicator's session, its handlers included, whatever `to` it names. An `owner` record names what his words are about with `--attr about=drive` or `--attr about=his work`, and the `record` tool, `record_lines` and `landing_guard` refuse one naming neither: one about the drive, drive tooling as the meta page defines it, his ruling on how the drive works included, goes to the meta session alone, which tells the coordinator what changes for its work; one about his work goes to the coordinator session hearing the order it serves, and the `record` tool, which `record_lines` and `landing_guard` make the only way the communicator sends one, keeps his words first as a new part of that order. An `answer`, `handoff`, `posted` or `refused` record goes to the session holding the order it serves, the meta session for a drive tooling order. The order a record serves is the one its `order` attr names, else its `job` or `waits` attr's, else its `thread`'s, so name `root@<drive>` and let it route; a record naming none keeps its `to`. A `defect` from any session goes to the meta session. The `record` tool and the cci `post` tool's hook address each so, and `landing_guard` refuses a `cci post` in Bash addressed otherwise, naming the flags to post it with.
- An agent of the communicator's, a handler included, posting to `communicator@<drive>` passes its own lane, such as `--lane handler` (`lane` on the `post` tool), since it posts as the communicator's lanes and cci refuses a record whose every `--to` is the lane posting it. A handler's own `claude --bg` session posts as those lanes because `drive handler` starts it with `CCI_PARENT_SESSION` naming the communicator's session, so cci binds that session to no lane, and with `CLAUDE_LONG_RUNNING_DRIVE` naming the drive, so a cci `post` or `find` with no `drive` field resolves the drive, since cci binds that session to no drive either.
- A record about a thread names it with `--attr thread=<ts>`. A record's text is one line of at most 120 characters, ids and paths in place of prose, because the channel puts it whole into the receiving session's context; the rest goes in the body, read with cci's `read` tool. No record carries a `max_words` attr: nothing reads it, his limits of 60 words a line and 120 a summary hold for every post, and `record_lines` drops one. A `report`'s attrs keep the names the drive reads: `catch_up`, `quiet`, `close`, `part`, `job`, `thread` and `waits`. The drive reads `catchup`, `catch-up` or `closes` as missing, so `record_lines` on the cci `post` tool and `landing_guard` on a Bash `cci post` refuse a key a separator, a capital or one letter from one of those and name the attr to use.
- `record_lines` refuses a `report` or `ask` that breaks its row below, naming the fix, every `ready` record, since `drive pr add` and the watch draw his PR card, and a reader's or designer's record to `owner`. It cuts a longer text at a word, ending `…`, with the whole text atop the body, then the body it carries, then the file its `path` attaches, which moves into the body, since the cci `post` tool keeps a body or a file but never both, as the drive's `record` tool does for a `defect`. It never cuts an `owner`, `answer` or `incident`, so his words stay whole.
- Only an open record, such as an `ask`, takes `--resolves`; answer an `owner` or `report`, which opens nothing, with `--re <seq>`.

| `report` | a coordinator, the meta session, the communicator's agents under their own lane, or the watch closing an incident whose monitors read OK and whose fix is live, owing an open incident with news its line after five quiet minutes, or writing his catch-up | `communicator@<drive>` | the facts of one post, opening `POST` for a handler to post or `FYI` for context, and the thread it goes in as `--attr thread=<ts>`, or an alert thread's link in its facts. A report names the thread its post goes in, which the post adds to the threads of each order the report is about; a `thread` that is no Slack ts is refused, and a post at his DM root leaves it out. His catch-up, an open incident's quiet line and the new messages in a thread an order watches are the reports no session writes, each started on a handler by the drive's watch, so the handler tool refuses the communicator's brief for one as a copy while that handler works: a thread an open order watches gets one with `--attr watch=true`, that order and that thread once a minute while new messages from anyone but him, the drive and a bot land there, naming each for the handler to answer there; each thread of open incidents five minutes past its last line with news, a job of them at work or an order, job or PR of them moved since that line, gets one with `--attr quiet=true`, its first incident's order and its thread, naming every incident there with its orders, jobs and PRs, each PR as the watch last read it from GitHub, what moved and which jobs are at work, for the handler to read, and his catch-up one with `--attr catch_up=true` and no thread, for his DM root, which `say` titles `*Catch-up since <when he was last heard>*` and closes it with a count of every open card waiting on him, linking the dashboard's queue, and each card asked since he was last heard, five a message, so its facts name no card and nothing waiting on him. The facts give one plain line per feature saying what changed for him, each thing named by its proper technical term, never a step or job name he has not used ("the roll press") or a vague stand-in. Each PR, fix, release, build, alert or thread he could open carries its URL, so the post links it where named, and each item waiting on him its card's permalink or `waits` id. A `POST` is at most 60 words, 120 with two bold feature lines; it names no job, order or `waits` id or record seq, which he cannot open; the body names `drive job report <j>` for each job whose kept return holds the facts, never copying them. Every `POST` but his catch-up names what its post finishes, and the `record_lines` hook on the cci `post` tool, or `landing_guard` on `cci post` in Bash, refuses one naming none of these: `--attr close=<o,…>` names each order the post finishes, comma-separated, its only order attr, since the handler closes what `close` names and drive refuses any other; `--attr part=<o>:<part>` names each part the post delivers, items separated by `;`, as `o491:fix;o504:the cause`, so one post in a thread two orders share delivers on both, and two parts of one order go as `o769:the release;o769:why the footers missed it`; it is the one attr a post reads parts from, so both hooks refuse a report carrying a part in any other, such as `part2`, `parts` or an attr whose value is `<o>:<part>`, naming the `part` value that carries them all; an item with no `<o>:` being a part of the order the report is about, its `order`'s, its `job`'s or else the newest in its thread; a report carrying a job's answer names its part, an incident's first check or cause among them, but never an open incident's `fix` before its fix is recorded live, by the release shipping its fix job's change or a builder on it returning `DONE`, since the incident lasts until its fix runs where it hurt: both hooks refuse that item, and only a report after that record or one closing the incident delivers it; an item naming an order or part the drive does not hold is refused, and the last parts not yet delivered of an open order need `close` naming that order too, except on an incident, which closes when its fix ships; `--attr progress=true` marks a progress line, which delivers no part and closes nothing. `--attr job=<j>` names the job whose answer it carries, which alone delivers nothing. A report delivers or closes something only on an order its writer's session holds, the coordinator's, the meta session's or the communicator's, and `say`, `drive order posted` and `drive order set --state done` share that one check, refusing another session's report and naming the holder's report to pass. | a `POST` gets a handler that writes and posts it with `say(order=<o>, record=<its seq>)`, which records it on the order and delivers each part its `part` attr names, an open incident's `fix` only once the watch recorded it live, on that order and each other order an item names, or each part of an order its `close` attr names, so a progress line keeps his promised time and delivers no part. It then closes each order `close` names with `drive order set <o> --state done --record <seq>`, an order with no thread included, such as a drive tooling order the drive added with no words of his: its line goes at his DM root. An `FYI` is kept as context. |
