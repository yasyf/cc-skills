# Between the sessions

- A session posts its own `owner`, `answer` or `defect`, and a handler his `owner` or `answer`, with the drive's `record` tool, which picks its lane and addresses the session it goes to, or the lane its `to` names, refusing a lane no session of the drive reads. An `answer` naming `waits` goes to the coordinator session hearing that job's order. Once an `owner` or `answer` record of his words on an open card of his goes out, named by its `waits` id, the ask seq in `re`, `thread_card` or his permalink in the card's thread, the tool closes that question card in the same call: it settles for his answer, showing his words, and is withdrawn for his other words, both through one `card-answer` call. A PR card stays up: only the coordinator's `--rework <PR>` on his order or on the job carrying it withdraws it. It refuses an `answer` naming a card that no longer waits on him, since his answer there is recorded already. `record_lines` refuses such a record on the cci `post` tool and `landing_guard` one by `cci post` in Bash, naming the tool. Every other record, and any other from an agent, goes through cci's `post` tool, whose `record_lines` hook checks it and `cci post` in Bash skips. Every session and agent posts, reads and closes records with the cci MCP tools (`post`, `read`, `close`) whenever its cci server is connected, and `landing_guard` refuses `cci post` in Bash while it is, by his words "metadata session is using the CLI insrqad of mcp for `cci post`"; `cci post` in Bash is for a session whose cci server is gone. While cci is down, or the receiving session's cci server dropped so it hears no channel record, a session reaches another with ListAgents, then SendMessage to `<drive>-<role>`, such as `<drive>-meta`, and posts the record once cci is back; the `record` tool's refusal names that route. A disconnect is never a reason a message did not go, by his words "use regualr claude code sesion messaging until it sback then there sno excuse".
- On cci, name the writer with `--lane communicator`, `--lane root` or `--lane meta`, since cci's default is never `root`, and address `root@<drive>`, `communicator@<drive>` or `meta@<drive>`, since a bare lane no session here reads can land on another drive. The meta session reads `meta@<drive>` at cursor `meta`.
- A sub-coordinator, the coordinator session for one project, writes as `--lane <lane>` and is addressed at `<lane>@<drive>`, `<lane>` being the project's name lowercased with each run of other characters a dash (`Iris` is `iris`, `Sand SQL` is `sand-sql`). Its block names the lane, it reads at cursor `coordinator-<lane>`, and its project's watch lines reach it there.
- The drive routes an `owner`, `answer`, `posted` or `refused` record from the communicator's session, its handlers included, to the session hearing the order it serves, the meta session for an order it holds: by its `job` or `waits` attr, else by its `thread`, whatever `to` it names. So name `root@<drive>` and let it route; a record naming neither keeps its `to`. A `defect` from any session goes to the meta session, or to the communicator while no meta session is bound, and an `owner` record carrying a `ruling` attr reaches the meta session as well. The `record` tool and the cci `post` tool's hook address each so, and `landing_guard` refuses a `cci post` in Bash addressed otherwise, naming the flags to post it with.
- An agent of the communicator's, a handler included, posting to `communicator@<drive>` passes its own lane, such as `--lane handler` (`lane` on the `post` tool), since it posts as the communicator's lanes and cci refuses a record whose every `--to` is the lane posting it. A handler's own `claude --bg` session posts as those lanes because `drive handler` starts it with `CCI_PARENT_SESSION` naming the communicator's session, so cci binds that session to no lane, and with `CLAUDE_LONG_RUNNING_DRIVE` naming the drive, so a cci `post` or `find` with no `drive` field resolves the drive, since cci binds that session to no drive either.
- A record about a thread names it with `--attr thread=<ts>`. A record's text is one line of at most 120 characters, ids and paths in place of prose, because the channel puts it whole into the receiving session's context; the rest goes in the body, read with cci's `read` tool. No record carries a `max_words` attr: nothing reads it, his limits of 60 words a line and 120 a summary hold for every post, and `record_lines` refuses one. A `report`'s attrs keep the names the drive reads: `catch_up`, `close`, `part`, `job`, `thread` and `waits`. The drive reads `catchup`, `catch-up` or `closes` as missing, so `record_lines` on the cci `post` tool and `landing_guard` on a Bash `cci post` refuse a key a separator, a capital or one letter from one of those and name the attr to use.
- `record_lines` refuses a `report`, `ready` or `ask` that breaks its row below, naming the fix, and a reader's or designer's record to `owner`. It cuts a longer text at a word, ending `…`, with the whole text atop the body, or alone when an attached file holds the rest, as the drive's `record` tool does for a `defect`. It never cuts an `owner`, `answer` or `incident`, so his words stay whole.
- Only an open record, such as an `ask`, takes `--resolves`; answer an `owner` or `report`, which opens nothing, with `--re <seq>`.

| Record | From | To | Carries | The receiver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `owner` | communicator, or a coordinator for words he typed in its terminal | `root@<drive>`, which the drive routes as below; `communicator@<drive>` from a coordinator | his words verbatim; his message's permalink as `--url`; his thread; `--attr ruling=<answer id>` on a ruling; `thread_card` for his words under a question card | makes his words orders, or applies a ruling to the live work by its table, while the meta session starts its skill rewrite; the communicator takes one from the coordinator as his message |
| `answer` | communicator | `root@<drive>`, which the drive routes as below | `--re` the `ask` whose card he answered, the seq its `waits` id ends in, and `--attr job=<j>`, which the `record` tool fills from `waits`; his pick or his own words; `undone` on an Undo tap. The ask closed when its card went up, so only this record says he answered, and the `record` tool settles the card as it sends it. | records his answer and resumes that job |
| `defect` | any session or its jobs | `meta@<drive>`, or `communicator@<drive>` while no meta session is bound | a tool defect it hit, any refusal verbatim, or a job whose brief serving drive tooling gave its order to the meta session, with its `Read your brief first:` line. A handler returns a `DEFECT` line instead, so only one fix starts; `record_lines` refuses its `defect`. | starts a drive tooling reader on that tool's repository, and its builder once the reader's research is kept; for a job its brief gave it, that job's builder on that line, with no order or reader of its own |
| `handoff` | the watch, as lane `watch`; a handler of the communicator's, as lane `handler` | `meta@<drive>`, or `communicator@<drive>` for an order the communicator holds | one watch line about an order that session holds that needs a step, such as a red check, his Approve tap, a `FREE` or an `ORPHAN`; from a handler, with `--attr job=<j>`, the result of a drive tooling agent the communicator started before the meta session bound | starts a builder briefed with the record to do that step, or acts on the result as on its own agent's; the coordinator never hears these lines, since the order is not its |
| `report` | a coordinator, the meta session, or the communicator's agents under their own lane | `communicator@<drive>` | the facts of one post, opening `POST` for a handler to post or `FYI` for context, and the thread or alert link it goes in. A report names one of its order's threads, the only place a post keeps his promised time, and one with `close` or `part` naming another is refused; `drive order set <o> --thread <ts>` adds one. His catch-up carries `--attr catch_up=true` and no thread, for his DM root, and `say` titles it `*Catch-up since <when he was last heard>*` and closes it with every open card waiting on him, five a message, so its facts name no card and nothing waiting on him. The facts give one plain line per feature saying what changed for him, each thing named by its proper technical term, never a step or job name he has not used ("the roll press") or a vague stand-in. Each PR, fix, release, build, alert or thread he could open carries its URL, so the post links it where named, and each item waiting on him its card's permalink or `waits` id. A `POST` is at most 60 words, 120 with two bold feature lines; it names no job, order or `waits` id or record seq, which he cannot open; the body names `drive job report <j>` for each job whose kept return holds the facts, never copying them. Every `POST` but his catch-up names what its post finishes, and the `record_lines` hook refuses one naming none of these: `--attr close=<o,…>` names each order the post finishes, comma-separated, its only order attr, since the handler closes what `close` names and drive refuses any other; `--attr part=<name>` names the part the post delivers, a report carrying a job's answer included, an incident's first check or cause among them, and a part that is the last one not yet delivered of the open order it is about, its `job`'s or else the newest in its thread, needs `close` naming that order too, except on an incident, which closes when its fix ships; `--attr progress=true` marks a progress line, which delivers no part and closes nothing. `--attr job=<j>` names the job whose answer it carries, which alone delivers nothing. A report delivers or closes something only on an order its writer's session holds, the coordinator's, the meta session's or the communicator's, and `say`, `drive order posted` and `drive order set --state done` share that one check, refusing another session's report and naming the holder's report to pass. | a `POST` gets a handler that writes and posts it with `say(order=<o>, record=<its seq>)`, which records it on the order and delivers the part its `part` attr names, or each part of an order its `close` attr names, so a progress line keeps his promised time and delivers no part. It then closes each order `close` names with `drive order set <o> --state done --record <seq>`, an order with no thread, such as a drive tooling order, included: its line goes at his DM root. An `FYI` is kept as context. |
| `ready` | the watch, as lane `watch`, once per head when every PR of its native GitHub stack the drive tracks is green and approved with no hold and no rework owed, each checked against his words at its head by `drive job report` or `drive pr set --serves`; a coordinator sending one again after a refusal | `communicator@<drive>` | a stack's PR URLs, bottom first, and in its text the order it carries out, quoted for the line of at most 60 words above his card: `his order o<n>` and his words, or a drive tooling order's "Drive tooling: …" words with no thread, refused without either. It names only PRs serving one of those orders, never a port; `record_lines` refuses a PR the drive owes no tap on. | draws the stack's PR card |
| `ask` | a coordinator or the meta session | `communicator@<drive>` | the question as its text; `--attr ask=<the path drive job ask printed>` for this question, since each question stays open until his answer to its own card and the card draws from that file alone; `--attr job=<j>`. An ask for a job on an open incident order carries `incident=true` and, as its `thread`, the incident's, so its handler says in that thread the moment the card is up that the card blocks the incident: the cci `post` tool's hook adds both, and `landing_guard` refuses a `cci post` in Bash without them. `record_lines` and `landing_guard` refuse an ask with no `job`, or whose file the job keeps for another question or for one his answer closed, such as its earlier question's, so a job's new question is kept with `drive job ask` first, a remote lane's too, which nothing else keeps; never the block itself, a design fork's included. A block asking again, in the same words, what an open question of the job asks needs nothing more, since `drive job ask` keeps it as that question; add `--attr waits=<an open card's waits id>` only to redraw that card with a question in new words. | draws the question card `drive job card <file> --waits <drive>:<j>-<the record's seq>` prints, under the waits id its question already holds when the job asked it before, so one question keeps one card. cc-slack posts it at his DM root with its permalink in the order's thread under the card's question, where Slack draws the card. The communicator then closes the ask with a `posted` record and posts nothing else, since the findings stay in the file for Explain more. With `waits`, one call redraws that card in place, posting nothing, every older copy losing its buttons. `drive job card` refuses to redraw a card his words under it answered, because they are his answer. |
| `posted` | communicator | `root@<drive>` | `--resolves` the `ask` whose card is up; the card's permalink as `--url`; `--attr waits=<its waits id>` | nothing, since the coordinator's subscription leaves `posted` out. The card stays his open question in `drive view` and the watch until his `answer` arrives |
| `refused` | communicator | `root@<drive>`, or the session that wrote a held report | `--resolves` the `ask` it refuses, and why, quoted, such as the card cc-slack or the length hook refused; or, written by the `message_limits` hook when it holds a report's line while he is away, `--re` that report and `--attr held=<its seq>` with its order, naming each correction cci already holds re it | resumes that job with the refusal to fix its `ASK` and keep it again with `drive job ask`, then posts its new `ask`; on `held`, knows the report did not reach him and waits for the `EVENT catch-up` line that names it |
