# Between the sessions

- A session posts its own `owner`, `answer` or `defect`, and a handler his `owner` or `answer`, with the drive's `record` tool, which picks its lane and addresses the session it goes to, or the lane its `to` names, refusing a lane no session of the drive reads. An `answer` naming `waits` goes to the session holding that job's order, the meta session for a drive tooling order, else the coordinator session hearing it. Once an `owner` or `answer` record of his words on an open card of his goes out, named by its `waits` id, the ask seq in `re`, `thread_card` or his permalink in the card's thread, the tool closes that question card in the same call: it settles for his answer, showing his words, and is withdrawn for his other words, both through one `card-answer` call. A PR card stays up: only the holding session's `--rework <PR>` on his order or on the job carrying it withdraws it. It refuses an `answer` naming a card that no longer waits on him, since his answer there is recorded already. `record_lines` refuses such a record on the cci `post` tool and `landing_guard` one by `cci post` in Bash, naming the tool. Every other record, and any other from an agent, goes through cci's `post` tool, whose `record_lines` hook checks it and `cci post` in Bash skips. Every session and agent posts, reads and closes records with the cci MCP tools (`post`, `read`, `close`) whenever its cci server is connected, and `landing_guard` refuses `cci post` in Bash while it is, by his words "metadata session is using the CLI insrqad of mcp for `cci post`"; `cci post` in Bash is for a session whose cci server is gone. While cci is down, or the receiving session's cci server dropped so it hears no channel record, a session reaches another with ListAgents, then SendMessage to `<drive>-<role>`, such as `<drive>-meta`, and posts the record once cci is back; the `record` tool's refusal names that route. A disconnect is never a reason a message did not go, by his words "use regualr claude code sesion messaging until it sback then there sno excuse".
- On cci, name the writer with `--lane communicator`, `--lane root` or `--lane meta`, since cci's default is never `root`, and address `root@<drive>`, `communicator@<drive>` or `meta@<drive>`, since a bare lane no session here reads can land on another drive. The meta session reads `meta@<drive>` at cursor `meta`.
- A sub-coordinator, the coordinator session for one project, writes as `--lane <lane>` and is addressed at `<lane>@<drive>`, `<lane>` being the project's name lowercased with each run of other characters a dash (`Iris` is `iris`, `Sand SQL` is `sand-sql`). Its block names the lane, it reads at cursor `coordinator-<lane>`, and its project's watch lines reach it there.
- The drive routes an `owner`, `answer`, `posted` or `refused` record from the communicator's session, its handlers included, whatever `to` it names. An `owner` record names what his words are about with `--attr about=drive` or `--attr about=his work`, and the `record` tool, `record_lines` and `landing_guard` refuse one naming neither: one about the drive, drive tooling as the meta page defines it, his ruling on how the drive works included, goes to the meta session alone, which tells the coordinator what changes for its work; one about his work goes to the coordinator session hearing the order it serves. An `answer`, `posted` or `refused` record goes to the session holding the order it serves, the meta session for a drive tooling order. The order a record serves is its `job` or `waits` attr's, else its `thread`'s, so name `root@<drive>` and let it route; a record naming neither keeps its `to`. A `defect` from any session goes to the meta session. The `record` tool and the cci `post` tool's hook address each so, and `landing_guard` refuses a `cci post` in Bash addressed otherwise, naming the flags to post it with.
- An agent of the communicator's, a handler included, posting to `communicator@<drive>` passes its own lane, such as `--lane handler` (`lane` on the `post` tool), since it posts as the communicator's lanes and cci refuses a record whose every `--to` is the lane posting it. A handler's own `claude --bg` session posts as those lanes because `drive handler` starts it with `CCI_PARENT_SESSION` naming the communicator's session, so cci binds that session to no lane, and with `CLAUDE_LONG_RUNNING_DRIVE` naming the drive, so a cci `post` or `find` with no `drive` field resolves the drive, since cci binds that session to no drive either.
- A record about a thread names it with `--attr thread=<ts>`. A record's text is one line of at most 120 characters, ids and paths in place of prose, because the channel puts it whole into the receiving session's context; the rest goes in the body, read with cci's `read` tool. No record carries a `max_words` attr: nothing reads it, his limits of 60 words a line and 120 a summary hold for every post, and `record_lines` drops one. A `report`'s attrs keep the names the drive reads: `catch_up`, `close`, `part`, `job`, `thread` and `waits`. The drive reads `catchup`, `catch-up` or `closes` as missing, so `record_lines` on the cci `post` tool and `landing_guard` on a Bash `cci post` refuse a key a separator, a capital or one letter from one of those and name the attr to use.
- `record_lines` refuses a `report`, `ready` or `ask` that breaks its row below, naming the fix, and a reader's or designer's record to `owner`. It cuts a longer text at a word, ending `…`, with the whole text atop the body, or alone when an attached file holds the rest, as the drive's `record` tool does for a `defect`. It never cuts an `owner`, `answer` or `incident`, so his words stay whole.
- Only an open record, such as an `ask`, takes `--resolves`; answer an `owner` or `report`, which opens nothing, with `--re <seq>`.

The ask closed when its card went up, so only this record says he answered, and the `record` tool settles the card as it sends it. | `report` | a coordinator, the meta session, the communicator's agents under their own lane, or the watch closing an incident whose monitors read OK and whose fix is live or writing his catch-up | `communicator@<drive>` | the facts of one post, opening `POST` for a handler to post or `FYI` for context, and the thread it goes in as `--attr thread=<ts>`, or an alert thread's link in its facts. Its `--url` is only the link of what it measured, such as a trace, notebook, log or build, which `{record:}` links in his posts, so `record_lines` and `landing_guard` refuse a `--url` naming a message in his DM. A report names one of its order's threads, the only place a post keeps his promised time, and one naming another, a progress line included, is refused when written, but for the thread of his message the report answers, named by its `re`, which its post adds to the threads of each order the report is about; a `thread` that is no Slack ts is refused too, and a post at his DM root leaves it out. His catch-up is the one report no session writes: the drive's watch writes it with `--attr catch_up=true`, no thread and no `--to`, for his DM root, and starts the handler that posts it, so no session hears it, and `say` titles it `*Catch-up since <when he was last heard>*` and closes it with every open card waiting on him, five a message, so its facts name no card and nothing waiting on him. The facts give one plain line per feature saying what changed for him, each thing named by its proper technical term, never a step or job name he has not used ("the roll press") or a vague stand-in. Each PR, fix, release, build, alert or thread he could open carries its URL, so the post links it where named, and each item waiting on him its card's permalink or `waits` id. A `POST` is at most 60 words, 120 with two bold feature lines; it names no job, order or `waits` id or record seq, which he cannot open; the body names `drive job report <j>` for each job whose kept return holds the facts, never copying them. Every `POST` but his catch-up names what its post finishes, and the `record_lines` hook on the cci `post` tool, or `landing_guard` on `cci post` in Bash, refuses one naming none of these: `--attr close=<o,…>` names each order the post finishes, comma-separated, its only order attr, since the handler closes what `close` names and drive refuses any other; `--attr part=<o>:<part>` names each part the post delivers, items separated by `;`, as `o491:fix;o504:the cause`, so one post in a thread two orders share delivers on both, an item with no `<o>:` being a part of the order the report is about, its `order`'s, its `job`'s or else the newest in its thread; a report carrying a job's answer names its part, an incident's first check or cause among them, an item naming an order or part the drive does not hold is refused, and the last parts not yet delivered of an open order need `close` naming that order too, except on an incident, which closes when its fix ships; `--attr progress=true` marks a progress line, which delivers no part and closes nothing. `--attr job=<j>` names the job whose answer it carries, which alone delivers nothing. Every `POST`, a progress line included, also names in `part` each part of the orders it is about that a finished job answered and no post delivered yet, the parts the watch calls answered and not posted, or closes that order: both hooks run the one check that refuses a report leaving one out, naming the part, its job and the `--attr part` to add, and a part a post of the drive already delivered is marked first by the session holding the order with `drive order posted <o> <that post's link> --part "<part>"`. A report delivers or closes something only on an order its writer's session holds, the coordinator's, the meta session's or the communicator's, and `say`, `drive order posted` and `drive order set --state done` share that one check, refusing another session's report and naming the holder's report to pass. | a `POST` gets a handler that writes and posts it with `say(order=<o>, record=<its seq>)`, which records it on the order and delivers each part its `part` attr names, on that order and each other order an item names, or each part of an order its `close` attr names, so a progress line keeps his promised time and delivers no part. It then closes each order `close` names with `drive order set <o> --state done --record <seq>`, an order with no thread included, such as a drive tooling order the drive added with no words of his: its line goes at his DM root. An `FYI` is kept as context. |
| `ready` | the watch, as lane `watch`, once per head when every PR of its native GitHub stack the drive tracks is green and approved with no hold and no rework owed, each checked against his words at its head by `drive job report`, the watch's `drive pr check` or `drive pr set --serves`; a coordinator sending one again after a refusal | `communicator@<drive>` | a stack's PR URLs, bottom first, and in its text the order it carries out, quoted for the line of at most 60 words above his card: `his order o<n>` and his words, or a drive tooling order's "Drive tooling: …" words, refused without either. It names only PRs serving one of those orders, never a port; `record_lines` refuses a PR the drive owes no tap on. | draws the stack's PR card |
| `ask` | a coordinator or the meta session | `communicator@<drive>` | the kept question as its text, by `--path <the path drive job ask printed>` and no text, which cci reads as that file's first line, as the post hooks do; `--attr ask=<that path>` for this question, since each question stays open until his answer to its own card and the card draws from that file alone; `--attr job=<j>`. An ask for a job on an open incident order carries `incident=true` and, as its `thread`, the incident's, so its handler says in that thread the moment the card is up that the card blocks the incident: the cci `post` tool's hook adds both, and `landing_guard` refuses a `cci post` in Bash without them. `record_lines` and `landing_guard` refuse an ask with no `job`, or whose file the job keeps for another question or for one his answer closed, such as its earlier question's, so a job's new question is kept with `drive job ask` first, a remote lane's too, which nothing else keeps; never the block itself, a design fork's included. A block asking again, in the same words, what an open question of the job asks needs nothing more, since `drive job ask` keeps it as that question; add `--attr waits=<an open card's waits id>` only to redraw that card with a question in new words. | draws the question card `drive job card <file> --waits <drive>:<j>-<the record's seq>` prints, under the waits id its question already holds when the job asked it before, so one question keeps one card. cc-slack posts it at his DM root with its permalink in the order's thread under the card's question, where Slack draws the card. The communicator then closes the ask with a `posted` record and posts nothing else, since the findings stay in the file for Explain more. With `waits`, one call redraws that card in place, posting nothing, every older copy losing its buttons. `drive job card` refuses to redraw a card his words under it answered, because they are his answer. When his recorded words already settle the question, the communicator draws no card and closes the ask with an `answer` record by the `record` tool, its waits id and `ruling` naming the answer that settles it. |
