# Between the sessions

Records carry work between the drive's sessions. Three things check a record the same way: the drive's `record` tool, the `record_lines` hook on the cci `post` tool, and `landing_guard` on `cci post` in Bash. A refusal names the fix or the tool to use.

## Which tool sends a record

- The drive's `record` tool sends a session's own `owner`, `answer`, `handoff` or `defect`. It picks the lane and addresses the session the record goes to.
- Every other record, and any other from an agent, goes through cci's `post` tool. Sessions and agents post, read and close records with the cci MCP tools (`post`, `read`, `close`) while their cci server is connected, and `landing_guard` refuses `cci post` in Bash meanwhile. `cci post` in Bash is for a session whose cci server is gone.
- The communicator sends every record with the `record` tool and makes no other cci call but `cci drive use`.
- While cci is down, or the receiving session's cci server dropped, reach the session with ListAgents, then SendMessage to `<drive>-<role>`, such as `<drive>-meta`, and post the record once cci is back. The `record` tool's refusal names that route.
- An unfinished job's id is the lane that job hears, and only the `record` tool posts there. The record is held on the job with its seq and sender, as `drive job resume` holds a text, so its running worker or codex run gets it. A codex run's own `cci post` is checked against the drive its environment names and held on the job it names, so one codex job answers another.

## Lanes and addresses

- On cci, name the writer with `--lane communicator`, `--lane root` or `--lane meta`, since cci's default is not `root`. Address `root@<drive>`, `communicator@<drive>` or `meta@<drive>`, since a bare lane can land on another drive. A lane no session of the drive reads, such as `coordinator`, is refused. The meta session reads `meta@<drive>` at cursor `meta`.
- A sub-coordinator, the coordinator session for one project, writes as `--lane <lane>` and is addressed at `<lane>@<drive>`. `<lane>` is the project's name lowercased with each run of other characters a dash (`Billing` is `billing`, `Sand SQL` is `sand-sql`). It reads at cursor `coordinator-<lane>`, and its project's watch lines reach it there.
- `record_lines` posts each cci `post` call as its session's lane, whatever `lane` it names, so a record a job's agent writes reads as the session's that started it and replies reach that session. An agent's record whose every `to` is that lane goes out as the agent's own type, such as `builder`, since cci refuses a record addressed only to the lane posting it. A `cci post` in Bash names its own `--lane`.
- Every post the drive's own code makes, such as the watch's lines or `drive pr wake`'s handoff, sets `CCI_PARENT_SESSION`, which marks it as a post made for another session, so it binds the process posting it to no lane. An Orca worker or subagent posting as the lane of the session holding its order is not refused as that lane's second binder.

## Where the drive routes a record

A lane you name in `to` stands: you decide which session acts. Name meta for a defect or his words about the drive, and the session holding the order, or running the job, for his words about his work. A report to the communicator still goes to the posts lane the drive posts from. The rules below route only a record of the `record` tool that names no `to`. The order a record serves is the one its `order` attr names, else its `job` or `waits` attr's, else its `thread`'s. A record naming none keeps its `to`.

- An `owner` record names what it is about with `--attr about=drive` or `--attr about=his work`, and is refused with neither. One about the drive, his ruling on how the drive works included, goes to the meta session, which tells the coordinator what changes. One about his work goes to the coordinator session hearing the order it serves, and the `record` tool first keeps his words as a new part of that order.
- An `answer` naming `waits`, and an `answer`, `handoff` or `refused` record naming a job, goes to the coordinator session whose agent runs that job on this Mac, else the meta session for a drive tooling order, else the coordinator session hearing its order. One naming no job goes to the session that wrote the record its `--re` answers, else to the session holding the order it serves.
- A `defect` from any session goes to the meta session. Any other record from a session outside the communicator's goes to every lane its `to` names.
- A call whose `owner` or `answer` records name another drive's question card, by `waits` or `thread_card`, is kept on that drive and posted as its communicator lane, since his words under a card reach whichever drive's watch reads his DM. A call mixing two drives' records is refused.

## His words on a question card

A job's question in the drive decides its card.

- An `owner` or `answer` record of his words names the open question by its `waits` id, `thread_card`, the `topic` `card:<thread_ts>`, or his permalink under its card. The `record` tool ends that question in the same call, before the record goes out: answered for his answer, withdrawn for his owner words about that card's work. The card follows through one `card-answer` call, showing his words with no buttons. A call sent again after cci refused the record posts it and holds no builder.
- His feedback on the drive under a product card leaves the card asking.
- A record naming a `ruling` with its `url` also keeps that ruling as his drive answer and prints its listing: each other card asking him, undelivered part of an open order holding more than one, and PR not yet live it could change. The watch's `ANSWER` line chases the listing until `drive answer <id>` marks each, `--dropped` for each his words remove.
- His answer also ends, as superseded, each older open question of the same job it decides, as Opus reads it.
- His tap ends the question in the first write of cc-slack's card command, before its `answer` record posts. cc-slack reruns the command until the record stores, and the card shows the tap recorded only then.
- The tool refuses an `answer` naming only ended questions, since his answer is recorded already. One exception: his words under a card whose question his tap ended in the ten minutes before, by Slack ts, end it in place of the tap, and the card drops the tap and its Undo through `card-answer --replaces`. His tap or write-in on any copy of a card after the answer that ended it replaces that answer: the card command marks the earlier cc-notes answer superseded and redraws the question's other card copies.
- A PR card stays up through all of this. The holding session's `--rework <PR>=<his permalink>`, on his order or on the job carrying it, withdraws it.

## Shape

- A record's text is one line of at most 120 characters, ids and paths in place of prose, because the channel puts it whole into the receiving session's context. The rest goes in the body, read with cci's `read` tool. `record_lines` cuts a longer text at a word, ending `…`, and puts the whole text atop the body, then the body, then the file its `path` attaches, since the cci `post` tool keeps a body or a file but not both. It does not cut an `owner`, `answer` or `incident`.
- A record about a thread names it with `--attr thread=<ts>`.
- An open record takes `--resolves`. An `owner` or `report` opens nothing, so answer one with `--re <seq>`. A close with no `to` goes to the lanes that wrote the records it closes.
- A `report`'s attrs keep the names the drive reads: `close`, `part`, `progress`, `milestone`, `job`, `thread`, `waits`, `until`, `watch`, `incident`, which only an open incident order's report carries, and `customer_outage`, which marks a report on a customer team's outage the drive did not cause. A key a separator, a capital or one letter from one of those, such as `closes`, is refused, naming the attr to use. `record_lines` drops a `max_words` attr, which nothing reads.
- `record_lines` refuses an `ask` record carrying his card's ASK block, a first line opening `ASK `, or an `ask` or `waits` attr, since `drive job ask` draws his question card. A question to another session's lane is a cci `ask` with none of those. It refuses every `ready` record, since `drive pr add` and the watch draw his PR card, and a reader's or designer's record to `owner`.

## Reports

- A report opens `POST` for the drive to post, or `FYI` for context. Its words after `POST` hold the facts of the post; its `thread` and `channel` attrs name where it belongs. It names the thread its post goes in as `--attr thread=<ts>`, or carries an alert thread's link in its facts. A post at his DM root leaves `thread` out, and a `thread` that is no Slack ts is refused. The post adds that thread to the threads of each order the report is about.
- Every `POST` reaches him, so you decide when a line is worth his read. Send `POST` when it closes an order or delivers a part (`close` or `part`), marks a milestone the watch does not post itself (`milestone=true`), such as a release starting, ending or failing, or reports a customer team's outage (`customer_outage=true`). Send a `progress=true` `POST` only when it answers a message of his, is on an open incident, is on an order he set a cadence on with `--every`, answers teammates in a thread an order watches, or posts at a channel root outside his DM. Any other progress is an `FYI` report: it stays on the dashboard and in the records. His words: "clearly these reports are useless / not valuable / spammy, so remove them entirely or rethink the whole model".
- A `POST` asks for one post that can go out now. A later step stays in the sender's own wait, which sends a fresh `POST` when it falls due.
- The facts give one plain line per feature saying what changed for him, each thing by its proper technical term, with the URL of each PR, fix, release, build, alert or thread he could open, and the card permalink or `waits` id of each item waiting on him. A `POST` is at most 60 words, 120 with two bold feature lines. It names no job, order or `waits` id or record seq, which he cannot open. The body points at `drive job report <j>` for each job whose kept return holds the facts.
- Its `--url` is the link of what it measured, such as a trace, notebook, log or build, which `{record:}` links in his posts. A `--url` naming a message in his DM is refused.
- Every `POST` names what its post finishes, and is refused naming none of these:
  - `--attr close=<o,…>` names each order the post finishes, comma-separated. The communicator closes each with `drive order set <o> --state done --record <seq>`, an order with no thread included: its first line goes at his DM root and starts the thread its later lines reply under.
  - `--attr part=<o>:<part>` names each part the post delivers, items separated by `;`, as `o491:fix;o504:the cause`. Two parts of one order go as `o769:the release;o769:why the footers missed it`. An item with no `<o>:` is a part of the order the report is about: its `order`'s, its `job`'s, or else the newest in its thread. It is the one attr parts are read from, so a part in any other attr, such as `part2` or `parts`, is refused. An item naming an order or part the drive does not hold is refused. The last undelivered parts of an open order need `close` naming that order too, except on an incident, which closes when its fix ships.
  - `--attr milestone=true` marks a milestone he acts on or wants to know, which delivers no part and closes nothing.
  - `--attr progress=true` marks a progress line, which delivers no part and closes nothing.
- `--attr job=<j>` names the job whose answer the report carries. Alone it delivers nothing: a report carrying a job's answer names its part.
- An open incident's `fix` part is delivered only once its fix is recorded live, by the release shipping its fix job's change or a builder on it returning `DONE`, since the incident lasts until its fix runs where it hurt. An earlier report naming it is refused. Its first check and its cause are parts a report may deliver before then.
- A report marked `incident=true` whose orders hold no open incident is refused, naming `drive incident open`, `drive order set <o> --incident` and `customer_outage=true`, since every check reads the order's incident flag.
- A report delivers or closes something only on an order its writer's session holds. `say`, `drive order posted` and `drive order set --state done` share that check and name the holder's report to pass.
- The watch posts these itself from the drive's rows through `say`: the close report of a proved order, by `drive job prove`, a `DONE` at a merge closing an order with no teammate reporter, a followed build's end line, an incident's Live line, the daily budget line and the question card asking him about a job a model's safeguards stopped three times. A drive tooling order a session opened that has posted nothing gets no close report: `drive job prove` closes it on its proof, or `drive order set <o> --state done --proof <its proof>` does, and a report closing one is refused.
- A `withdraw`, sent with the cci `post` tool `re` the report's seq, takes back a report not yet posted. A report `re` an earlier report's seq goes out as a correction, and the communicator rewords that post.

## Posts outside his DM

- A post at the root of a channel outside his DM, such as a staging claim, or a line in its thread, names the end of what it claims as `--attr until=<RFC 3339 time with its offset>`. `say` keeps it on the order the report is about, and a later line in that thread naming a new `until` moves it. An `until` with no offset is refused, and `say` refuses one on a post anywhere else.
- A post at that root goes out only on a report naming an `until` still to come. `say` sends any other back to its writer as a `refused` record re the report, and posts nothing.
- Once such a post is 5 minutes old, the watch reads its thread once and sends the order's holder a `DUE` line. It says the claimed work may start, or quotes each reply there from anyone but the drive and a bot, which a `POST` in that thread answers before that work starts.
- From the `until` time the watch sends the order's holder a `DUE` line every 10 minutes until a drive post is in that thread, its closing line.
- What a claim and its closing line say is the repository's rule, such as the `staging-claim` rule in its `.claude/long-running.toml`.

## Reports the watch writes

No session writes these.

- `--attr watch=true`: a thread an open order watches, once a minute while new messages from anyone but him, the drive and a bot land there, naming each for the communicator to answer there.
- The watch hands the communicator each record to post, and each message of his unanswered for 10 minutes, as one cci `note` from lane `watch` opening `ANSWER THIS:`. The note is keyed by its brief, so a pass repeating it sends nothing new.
- The watch also writes the report that closes an incident whose monitors read OK and whose merged fix PRs run in production.
