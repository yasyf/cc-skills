## Roles and architecture

On a drive where many lanes open PRs, `landing-desk` takes lane reports and reconciles landings so the coordinator does not wait on them. Spawn `landing-desk` before any lane that opens a PR whenever three or more lanes ship through one merge queue or the drive outlives one context window. Below that threshold, the opening lane lands its own PR, or the coordinator lists it for the label watch in `reference/watchers.md`, with no desk.

The supervisor's landing pass (`lr landing pass`) is the drive's merge queue and owns D3, D14, D16, and D20 across all shards. The landing desk never enqueues beside it. The coordinator owns holds under D3 with `cci post --kind hold --pr <n>` and a typed `--resolves`, receives `P0` and `RULING NEEDED` lines immediately, and receives a summary every 30 minutes.

`scripts/ledger.py` is the desk's one tool, applying R5, and R3 in `SKILL.md` to PR state and watching. It uses `ccx vcs pr state` for refreshes and `ccx vcs pr watch` for transitions; both read ccx's machine-wide pull request cache, which polls each repository at most once every 30 seconds. The PR store is a cc-notes ledger with one row per PR our lanes shipped, holding `lane`, `declared_intent`, holds, routing, label history, and landing fields on `refs/cc-notes/*`. Lane messages are cci records keyed `#<seq>` on the drive named by `runner.json` `drive`; owner asks are `ask/<seq>` rows beside the PR rows. cc-notes finds the ledger through the working directory's repository, so a lane outside that checkout passes `ledger.py -C <checkout> <verb>`. Once `drive.py start` registers the drive, every `ledger.py` verb resolves `--ledger` and `--drive` from `--drive <id or cci drive>` when given, else from the session's drive.

Scripts are on `PATH` by name, as SKILL.md describes.

The desk grades, lands, and tracks only through `ledger.py`, never scripts of its own; a gap in `ledger.py` is a `RULING NEEDED`, not a workaround. `reference/landing-desk-brief.md` is the desk's spawn brief.

## Rules D1 to D23

**D1. The landing desk is the only enqueuer; lanes report; the coordinator owns holds and priority PRs.** A lane never runs `stack-enqueue` outside `--check` and `--status`, `gt merge`, `gh pr merge`, or `ledger.py label`, and never adds a `merge` or `merge-fast` label. The pack's `lane_landing` hook reads the drive registry and warns on those commands against the drive's repo without blocking them from every lane except `landing-desk`, its shards, priority desks, `desk-runner`, `merge-train` lanes, and incident lanes. An incident lane is named `incident-` or `outage-`, briefed with `ccx: incident=<id>` other than `none`, or named `<x>-fix` and briefed with `Register slice: lane=<x>-fix step=incident`; the hook warns it only on `stack-enqueue` and `gh pr merge --admin` and lets it run `gh pr merge <n> --squash`. The same pack warns on `tools/ci trunk dequeue`, `restart`, and `prioritize` from every lane, desks included, because only the coordinator steers the queue. A tooling lane merges its own PRs in other repos.

The moment a PR is green, the lane reports `"READY"` with `--verdict clean` on `ledger.py report --drive <drive>` and keeps `ccx vcs pr watch` on the stack. On ejection or conflict, the lane restacks at once with `lr landing restack --runner <runner config> --lane <lane> <top PR>`, which replays the stack on a Sprite, pushes, and posts each new head; the lane resolves by hand only a conflict that tool reports, and the runner re-enqueues it. When a coordinator `go` names the conflicting head, the landing pass runs that same restack itself under the lane's name, once per head, and the lane hears of the conflict only when the replay moved nothing. A lane is not finished until its squash `(#N)` is on the base branch; after a bottom prefix lands, it restacks the PRs above it the same way with no ruling needed.

**D2. Track and grade our lanes' PRs.** The pack's PR hook, `ledger.py report`, `ledger.py register`, and an explicit `refresh --pr` are the only paths that open a PR row. `refresh` admits every open PR on a branch prefix or stack a lane registered, and the landing pass registers a PR carrying the `drive:<drive>` label under its head branch. `ledger.py unadmit --pr <n>` withdraws a PR admitted by mistake; its row stays unregistered until a lane runs `register --pr` for it, and any merge label the desk set comes off.

Owner ask rows open only through `ledger.py ask`. The desk grades every tracked PR without waiting for a lane report at its current head, and never lists the repository's pull requests. The landing pass and `ledger.py label` refuse any stack holding a PR the drive did not admit; an `opened` record, a `head` record, or the author sign-in alone does not make a PR ours.

**D3. Enqueue every ready bottom prefix in priority order.** `<tip>` is the top of the largest contiguous bottom prefix whose PRs pass. Under the `label` adapter, `ledger.py label --pr <tip> --checkout <path>` walks base refs to the repo's default branch and re-reads every PR in that prefix. Each PR must be open with an approval in force on any commit that has not been dismissed or withdrawn, successful commit status, and no failed checks. It must also have a completed successful latest `ai-review`, no desk hold or lane `held` verdict on that head, no prior label or pull, and no conflict with its base. Allowed `mergeable_state` values are `clean`, `behind`, and `has_hooks`; on a legacy Graphite stack, a PR above the bottom may also read `unstable` while Graphite's `mergeability_check` is its only unfinished check.

`--expect-head` takes a 7-to-40-character lowercase hex prefix of the tip's sha and re-reads the tip immediately before grading. An untracked downstack PR or an orphaned base refuses the whole attempt. An open child outside the prefix is allowed only when a lane tracks its ledger row and its stack records it through the GitHub `stack` field on a native stack or the `Graphite / mergeability_check` check run on a legacy Graphite stack. Otherwise, link it with `ccx vcs stack submit` or retarget it to the trunk before adding the label. A lane report is not required; the forge decides whether a head is red or conflicting. When the repository carries a `stack-enqueue` script, each pass starts one `stack-enqueue <prefix top>` per ready prefix together in one Bash call, each backgrounded with `&`, then runs `wait` and collects each output. Each shard does the same for its lanes, using held rows from the whole ledger.

**D4. Landed means the squash is on the base branch.** `ledger.py reconcile` fetches the trunk once, lands every row a squash subject ending `(#n)` names, and settles each closed row left by tree equality. It does one `ccx vcs pr state` read over the rest, with per-PR forge calls only for a closed row no squash names, while `--dry-run` prints the changes and writes nothing. The queue deletes a stacked PR's base on landing, so `reconcile` never fetches that base and never settles a row by the PR's `merged` field. A closed row with neither match becomes `closed-without-squash`.

**D5. Route every red or conflicting row in the pass that finds it, once per head.** Run `refresh` then `route` in the same pass. `route` records the head, job, and lane it sent, so the same head and job are never routed twice and a moved head or a different job on the same head is routed again. Write nothing to the pull request. Route to a lane only while it is live; when a lane finishes, record it with `ledger.py gone` per R4 in `SKILL.md` and route its reds under D8.

**D6. Every hold has a reason and an expiry, and every message is recorded once.** `ledger.py hold` requires `--reason` and `--hours`; the summary excludes any row parked without them from its held count. `ledger.py enqueue` drops a second message with the same kind, PR, and head, so the ledger stores and answers duplicate idle notices only once.

**D7. The hot set lands by train.** A `merge-train` lane, spawned with the desk, owns every reported PR whose files hit the hot set, as `reference/merge-train-brief.md` details. Every two hours it fetches trunk, orders ready cars oldest first, and rebases them into one stack of at most six with `ccx vcs stack rebase --linearize`. It resolves each conflict once in the conflict workspace and enqueues the whole train once every car is green; a red car is fixed forward or ejected with `--parent`, never waited on. Route a hot-set row to the train, not to its lane.

**D8. Route ejections, conflicts, and reds in the pass that sees them.** The owning lane handles its own ejection from its watch. The desk routes an ejection or conflict to the lane at once when the watch or a pass shows it. A row whose lane is gone goes to `merge-train` for the hot set or the standing `red-desk` in that same pass. Hold a step red on trunk's latest build as `dev-red:<step>` with a 6-hour expiry instead of routing it. `ledger.py hold` refuses a PR that has green children unless `--stack` holds them all.

**D9. A stale plan is information, never a refusal.** A pull request's plan is computed at its head. When the base moves under a stack that plan reached, the desk prints the stacks and movers in the grade and labels anyway. The landing plans the tree it applies and refuses its own op classes there. A rebase is owed only for a merge conflict or after a bottom prefix lands under D16.

**D10. Record reports immediately; reconcile every three minutes.** The landing pass runs D3 every 180 seconds by default without waiting for a report. The landing desk types reports, runs refresh/watch/P0 and red routing, records holds, and keeps stale, summary, shards, and train duties.

Each desk pass reads all PR numbers in one `ccx vcs pr status <n1> <n2> ...` call and the Buildkite build list, never one REST call per PR, following the API budgets in `reference/watchers.md`. Stagger desks and shards by a minute at `:00`, `:01`, and `:02`. One refused prefix never stops another. A lane's `red` or `conflicting` verdict does not refuse a head the forge passes.

**D11. Name every clean row older than 30 minutes with its blocker.** `ledger.py stale` lists every open row whose latest report is `clean` and at least 30 minutes old. It prints one blocker per row, which is `held: <reason> until <stamp>`, `in the queue since <stamp>`, `in the queue, labelled outside the desk`, `routed: <job>`, `label refused: <reason>`, `head moved since the report`, or `never graded: run label --all-clean`. The summary carries the same lines and the p50 report-to-landing minutes. Clear each stale row's blocker in the pass that sees it with a label, route, hold, lift, or `"RULING NEEDED"`.

**D12. Shard at 15 lanes or 25 active rows, whichever comes first.** Split early. Spawn parallel `long-running:lane` sub-lanes named `landing-desk-<shard>`, each owning a named set of lanes' rows in the same ledger with `--shard lane-a,lane-b`. A stack's rows belong to the shard of its tip's lane.

`--shard` filters `refresh`, `landed`, `reconcile`, `route`, `label --all-clean`, `stale`, `summary`, and `inbox` to rows and cci messages whose `lane` is in the set. All shards share the ledger and its per-ledger refresh lock. Lanes keep reporting to `landing-desk`; the main desk types every message in and alone sends the coordinator the summary. When rows fall back under both thresholds, tell the shard lanes the drive is over for them.

**D13. Register each lane's stack when it starts and whenever it opens a PR.** The coordinator registers the drive with `drive.py start --ledger <id>`. The pack's PR hook registers every PR a drive session or Orca worker opens under the lane's name and records the head when the command prints one. `lr launch start` resolves the drive from the Orca run for workers launched by the relay. A session no drive claims prints one stderr line from `drive.py record`.

The hook settles the row through `drive.py landed` and `ledger.py landed` when a session posts `cci post --kind landed --pr <n>` or runs `gh pr close`. Lanes also register their branch at spawn with `ledger.py register --ledger <id> --lane <name> --branch-prefix <branch>`, ending in `/` or `-` for a stack namespace or else naming one exact branch.

Run from the lane's worktree, `register` records the worktree when `.git/gh-stack` or a legacy Graphite stack holds the branch, so `refresh` tracks branches `ccx vcs stack new` cuts later. `register` refuses a prefix that covers the owner's whole `<user>/` namespace or matches open PRs another lane tracks or that predate the ledger, naming those PRs and suggesting the exact branch, `<user>/<lane-slug>-`, or `--pr`. `ledger.py unregister --ledger <id> --lane <name>` removes a prefix and drops untouched rows only it pulled in; `ledger.py unregister --ledger <id> --pr <n>` removes a single PR row whole.

Hand-register a PR only when the hook's context line says it was not recorded. To move a PR to another lane, the coordinator runs `ledger.py reassign --ledger <id> --pr <n> --lane <new>`. Read recorded PRs with `ledger.py list --ledger <id> [--lane <name>] [--open] [--json]`. Each `refresh` makes one `ccx vcs pr state --repo <repo> <PR numbers> --lane-prefix <prefix>` call repeating `--lane-prefix` for each registered prefix.

**D14. Grade a moved or unreported head like any other.** Every tracked current head takes D3's gates without a report. The landing pass routes a `BLOCKED` head at most once per head, and only for red CI, a merge conflict, a changes-requested review or fidelity grade, or a queue ejection. It never routes a pending or running check, a missing approval, an ejection blaming a PR outside the stack, or a PR that is held, labeled `hold`, or stacked above a held PR. It also skips any PR based on another open PR's branch unless a non-gate status such as `buildkite/test` is red on its own head. A queue drop blaming a PR outside the stack re-enqueues the same head after the coordinator's next `go` record naming the PR, and a `flaky-check` drop goes to `landing.restack_lane`, never the PR's own lane.

The runner re-runs the gate immediately before creating the route and suppresses a blocker that already cleared, without re-reading at a later Orca delivery. When a merge conflict's lane has no active Orca dispatch and no session in `cci who`, but has a cci record in the last 60 minutes, the runner parks the conflict. The coordinator gets one `PARKED parked:<pr>` line per PR, and the conflict routes once the lane is back. Orca lanes receive a relay; other lanes receive `cci post --drive <drive> --lane landing-desk --kind blocker --topic <pr> --to <lane> --text "<blocker>"`. Under the `label --all-clean` fallback, send each refused head `new head <sha9>: <blocker>` once per head and blocker. If the head moved since the refresh, the next pass grades the new head without a route, while red CI and conflicts go through `route` without a duplicate message from the batch. The landing desk retains `ledger.py route` for red CI and conflicts and never repeats the runner's per-head gate messages.

**D15. Name what the desk is waiting on and ping the lane in the same pass.** The summary's `waiting:` line groups tracked open PRs as `ungraded` when the current head lacks a label and grade. That line marks them `refused` when the head has a label refusal with its reason in `stale` or `show`. A row reads `red` on a CI failure or `dirty`/`blocked` `mergeable_state`, and `held` on a desk hold or lane `held` verdict at the current head. Run `route` and send its messages in the same pass. `summary` settles landings first; `--checkout` defaults to the working directory and `--repo` to that checkout's origin.

**D16. The green bottom of a stack lands now.** Enqueue the largest contiguous bottom prefix whose PRs are green, approved, and unheld as one batch; never wait for the top of a stack to go green. After a native GitHub stack prefix merges atomically, inspect whether GitHub automatically rebased the remaining stack before routing an explicit restack. When a restack is needed, the landing pass verifies ledger `landed` rows and routes the first PR above the landed prefix by relay for Orca lanes or `cci post --drive <drive> --lane landing-desk --kind blocker --topic <pr> --to <lane> --text "<restack route>"` for others, then sets the route verified when the child's head moves or lands. The owning lane restacks the remaining PRs on a Sprite with `lr landing restack`, and the landing desk never duplicates this route.

**D17. An owner-visible change needs its render approved before the label.** Hold any PR that changes a UI, message, or generated document the owner sees rendered under D6 with reason `"render not approved"`. Keep that hold until the owner approves the exact render at the head being labeled, unless the owner granted ship-then-fix for that lane. A re-render at a new head requires fresh approval.

**D18. A queued PR is watched until its squash is on the base. An ejection is a P0 the pass it happens.** The desk arms `ledger.py watch --drive <drive>` under Monitor when it spawns and re-arms it on every expiry. It forwards every `P0` line to the coordinator immediately for same-turn action. Every lane that reports a PR runs `ccx vcs pr watch --lane-prefix <prefix> --until landed` under Monitor, re-armed on expiry, or in the foreground as `lr wait 540 cci:<drive>:<lane> -- ccx vcs pr watch --lane-prefix <prefix> --until landed` with `timeout: 570000`, never as a bare foreground command. `ejected` or `conflicting` means rebase now; the lane watches until its PRs land.

**D19. Land as many independent stacks at once as possible. Shape them so they do not conflict.** Every green, approved, unheld stack enqueues in the pass it reaches that state. Never hold a ready stack behind another stack's landing or slow down on an ejection. Shape stacks in the lane brief to avoid conflicts by putting a shared-file edit in the smallest additive first PR in the file's declared order and stacking on another open PR that touches the same file instead of racing it. Never commit a generated output; on ejection, rebase and regenerate it instead of resolving by hand.

**D20. A fast-tracked fix with a `Release-With` line, or any incident fix, starts its release in the pass it lands.** See [Incident fixes and release on landing](#incident-fixes-and-release-on-landing).

**D21. A format change lands readers first.** A PR that changes a format another build reads, such as a journal, seal, session, manifest, stored file, or row, is a writer. Ship its readers first in their own PR, and wait until that reader release is live on every reading client, including any browser bundle, desktop app, local daemon, or server, before enqueueing the writer. A client that never restarts on its own counts until it refuses the old build. The writer's body must carry `Readers-First: #<reader PR> live in <release>` for each reader and never a `Release-With` pairing it with its reader. The writer's lane posts `cci post --kind hold --pr <writer> --text "readers-first: #<reader> not live on every client"` when opening the writer, which holds it under D1 until the coordinator lifts it with `lift --resolves <seq>` naming the release and its live read-back. The landing desk grades each tracked PR's diff for a format change and posts a hold with reason `"readers-first: no reader named"` on any writer lacking a `Readers-First:` line.

**D22. A PR whose release must avoid a time window stays out of the queue until a release outside the window can carry it.** Every merged change ships in the next release anyone cuts. No hold record on any drive reaches another person's release. Hold the PR with `cci post --kind hold --pr <n>`, which refuses the enqueue under D1, until a release outside the window can carry it, or land it behind a code gate or feature flag that keeps it inert until then.

**D23. A PR that waits to go live names how it is safe on dev.** Its body may say it waits for a card, a later tap, a release, or a hold before it goes live, such as `lands only after #<n> is live`. That body must also name a code gate, a feature flag, or that the change is inert until a named caller lands, as in `nothing calls <verb> yet`. Without one, the landing pass refuses it with `UNSAFE-ON-DEV` to its lane and the coordinator, `lr landing go` and `lr landing gate` print the same reason, and its tap card lists it. The lane adds the gate to the code and names it in the body, or drops a wait that no longer holds.

## Queue adapters and runner passes

`lr landing pass` reaches the repository's Trunk queue through the `stack-enqueue` script `landing.queue_script` names in `lr.json`, and reads queue and CI state with `ccx vcs pr status`.

Each pass reads every open drive PR as facts and classifies each head into a state, a mover and a clock. A head that passes every gate enqueues as part of the largest clear bottom of its stack. A queued head whose head moved or that sits under an open hold leaves the queue, top first, with every PR stacked on it. An ejected head gets one resubmit and then goes to its lane as a red item.


When `landing.desk_lane` in `runner.json` names one or a list of lanes, the runner sends `QUEUED`, `LANDED`, `TAP-CARRIED`, `MERGE-ASK`, `BELOW-BLOCKED`, and `FORK-DEFERRED` records to that lane, each `landing.board_lane`, and the PR's owning lane, each read as its live successor, and not to the coordinator. Without `landing.desk_lane` they go to the coordinator. A ledger row that settles landed with no enqueue or squash of the runner's own gets its own `LANDED` record the pass the ledger reads its squash. A landed PR whose branch carried other open PRs into the trunk gets one `CARRIED` report to the coordinator and each `landing.board_lane`. That report lists every carried PR from the GitHub commits on the landed PR with the owner's tap, fidelity grade, and finder state. A `GO-UNQUEUED`, `GO-TAP-MISSING`, or `REFUSED` record goes to each `landing.go_lanes` lane, the desk lanes, and the owning lane, and to the coordinator only when no lane owns the PR. Every landing pass also posts one `state` record to the coordinator on topic `landing-state` with the queued, waiting, held, and refused counts.

A PR's owning lane for every blocker and restack the runner routes is the poster of the newest cci `head`, `opened`, `ready`, or `claim` record naming it, never the coordinator or a desk, else the lane that registered its ledger row. To hand a PR to a new lane, post a `claim` record as that lane. A cci blocker also goes to the coordinator when `cci who` lists no live session for that lane. A lane with no live session or dispatch and no cci record on the drive in the last 60 minutes is dead. Its blocker goes to `landing.restack_lane`, or the coordinator if none is named or all are dead, opening with `owner lane dead`, and its conflict is never parked.

## The check registry

Every prefix passes the check registry before it enqueues. `landing.checks` lists the checks in order, and every built-in runs when it lists none:

| Check | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `hold` | Reads the `hold` label and the PR's cci `hold` and `ready` records fresh just before the enqueue call. Runs first on every enqueue path, including the landing pass and `lr landing gate`. An incident record that only mentions a PR as context holds nothing. A `hold` holds exactly the PRs it was posted with as `--pr`; every `#<n>` in its text is prose, the leading one included. |
| `precondition` | Scans the PR body and cci records outside quotes, code, and block quotes for `lands only after`, `merge only after`, `before it lands`, `do not land until`, or `not for landing`. Never refuses a head; sends the coordinator one `note` warning per head quoting the sentence, including on incident squashes, so the coordinator can post a `hold` if it is a real dependency. |
| `rulings` | Runs `rulings.py check-pr` at the head, as detailed in the next section. |
| `fidelity` | Reads the fidelity lane's cci `review` grade at the head, as detailed in the next section. |
| `finder` | Reads the sol finder's cci `review` record at the head, as detailed in the next section. |
| `merge-mode` | Enforces `landing.merge_mode`, `landing.tap_rules`, and reserved rows at accept and again just before the enqueue call. |
| `approvals` and `required-ci` | The adapter's own verdict on the PR. Red CI counts only checks required by `dev`'s rulesets plus Trunk's queue check; unrequired checks such as `buildkite/release-pr-check` do not block or refuse. A stack check failing because `origin` lacks the PR's named branch refuses that head to its lane, naming the branch to push or retarget. |

The check that refuses a prefix posts its blocker or ask and stops it there. The pass posts no commit status and adds or removes no label, so a hand `gt merge`, web-UI enqueue, or hand adapter call bypasses every gate. `lr board waits --pr <n>` says what a PR waits on, and `lr landing gate <n>...` prints each gate a hand enqueue would pass over, both without posting anything. A failing required check posts one `blocker` per PR, refusal, red job, and cause, where the cause is the status description with run numbers and durations folded. A later head failing the same way gets one `note` under that blocker, while a changed cause or new red job opens a new blocker.

Each exact set of prefix heads gets one enqueue action. The runner permits another attempt for the same heads only when earlier attempts are proven to have enqueued nothing, or when `ccx vcs pr status` reads a believed-queued PR `not queued` or `evicted` at the admitted head. On ejection, the adapter's verdict, and never PR comment text, classifies the drop:
- `conflict`: stays out at that head, and the gate routes its ejection to the lane.
- `own-red`: gated again, and the adapter blocks it until a new head.
- `batch-mate-red`: gated again and re-enqueued at the same head; one blaming a PR outside the PR's own stack logs `AWAITS-GO` and waits for a coordinator `go` naming the PR.
- `flaky-check`: a red check that read green on the PR's own head and failed only in a queue draft holding nothing but its stack. It stays out at that head after its first ejection, sends one blocker naming the failing job to `landing.restack_lane` or the coordinator, and re-enters only at a new head.

When a batch leaves PRs out, the runner sends the coordinator `HELD-OUT`, `BATCH-WAITS`, or `FORK-DEFERRED`. `HELD-OUT` means a hold covers a PR the check sweeps into the batch. That PR and every PR above it stay out, the enqueue names only the tops below it with `--partial` on `graphite-stack-enqueue`, and no check or enqueue call ever names a held PR. Under `BATCH-WAITS`, a coordinator `go #<n> at <sha> with #<a> #<b>` binds its PRs and the `with` PRs into one batch, and the prefix holds only part of it because one is held, red, or untracked. Every PR of the batch and above stays out until the whole batch can enqueue in one call. With `FORK-DEFERRED`, the batch sits on an open fork outside it and waits until that fork lands, naming the waiting PRs and the fork. A batch that forks within itself goes in as one stack-enqueue call per linear chain in the same pass, bottom chain first.

## Fidelity, finder, and rulings checks

**Fidelity check.** `landing.fidelity_lane` in `runner.json` names one lane or a list of lanes; the runner re-reads it on every use, and the landing desk refuses to start without it and posts `FIDELITY-LANE-UNSET`. The runner reads cci `review` records from those lanes matching `--pr <owner/repo>#<n>` or bare `#<n>` whose text starts `fidelity: ok` or `fidelity: missing` and whose first short sha is the PR's current head, such as `fidelity: ok F307 at 9c0b891`. Each lane's newest grade at the head stands for that lane. A `missing` blocks until that same lane grades the head `ok`, a coordinator record about the PR resolves it, or a coordinator record reads `fidelity-override #<n> at <sha>` where `#` and `at` are optional and any id such as `174488`, `174488@f3c89519`, or the drive copy's seq may follow the colon. An `ok` counts once posted; register rows added later never void it. A head graded `missing`, graded `ok` only at an older head, or ungraded gets one cci `blocker` per head and grade to the PR's lane and the fidelity lanes. The prefix is cut below it, enqueueing passing PRs below with `--partial` and naming the cut in the blocker. The check runs again just before the enqueue call.

**Finder check.** The newest `finder:` review record naming the head from `finder-desk` or a numbered successor must read `finder: ok`; a lane's own record or a reply never grades a head. A head with none or whose newest is `finder: major` gets one `FINDER-PENDING #<n>` blocker per head to its lane and `finder-desk`. A finder pass answers a major with a new head's record or a later `finder: ok <head7> answered: <how>`, and a coordinator `finder-override #<n> at <sha>` settles a finder major at that head the same way as `fidelity-override`. The stall watch reads overrides the same way, and a coordinator `decide` whose override omits the PR or the head draws one `OVERRIDE-REFUSED` blocker to the coordinator.

**Restack grade carry.** When no grade names the current head, the runner compares `git patch-id --stable` of the PR's own commits at the current head and at any older head whose newest grades are `ok`. It reads those commits from the GitHub compare of `<head>~<n>...<head>` with `n` as the PR's commit count, or diffs against the PR's base; a compare GitHub refuses logs `PATCH-READ-FAILED` once and carries nothing. Equal patch ids carry the `ok` in that pass as `fidelity: ok at <new head>, patch-identical to <graded head>` under the graded record's cci seq. A fidelity lane asked to grade such a head answers immediately with the same line without re-reading. The newest `finder: ok` carries the same way.

**Runner-side finder and fidelity pipelines.** When `runner.json` carries `"finder": {"model": "sol", "concurrent": 2, "checkout": "<repo checkout>", "to": ["<lane>"], "timeout_minutes": 60}` and `lr.json` lists `grading`, `lr supervisor run` starts a sol finder pass on each `head` record, reading multiple `head:<pr>` attributes on one record as distinct heads. Each head retries up to 3 times before posting `FINDER-FAILED #<n>` to the coordinator and `to`, a desk with pending heads and no completion for 15 minutes posts `FINDER-DESK-STALLED`, and a coordinator record reading `finder: whole` switches the desk to whole-program mode; see `reference/finder-desk-brief.md`. When `runner.json` carries `"fidelity": {"model": "sol", "concurrent": 6, "baseline": "<live old-app baseline>", "to": ["<lane>"], "timeout_minutes": 60}` and `landing.fidelity_lane` includes `fidelity-pipeline`, the runner starts a fidelity pass on each `head` and `opened` record. The `fidelity` object's optional `service_touches` list holds `{"service": "<name>", "path": "<glob>", "added": "<regexp>"}` entries: an added non-test line under a path whose leading directories match `path` and whose text matches `added` makes the pass grade `old-app: CHANGES` and name that file, line and service, whatever the read said, and an entry whose glob or regexp does not compile is logged and skipped. An entry `{"service": "old-app pages", "path": "dashboard/src/iris/durable", "added": "\\S"}` covers the directory old pages run in `Forge-AI/monorepo`. A `reader` verdict on a head whose old-app line names such touches posts `fidelity: reader <head> OLD-APP-PENDING: <why>` to the lane and `to` only, never to every fidelity desk, and the head holds in grading until a fidelity desk posts a grade at that head. A coordinator `decide` reading `class wrong` re-runs the classifier, a `fidelity: reader <head> <why>` record grades a reader PR, and three failed attempts post `FIDELITY-FAILED #<n>` to the coordinator and `to`; see `reference/fidelity-desk-brief.md` and `reference/fidelity-job-brief.md`. Both pipelines address each `to` lane by its stem, the name without a trailing `-<n>`, so a stale number in the config still reaches that lane's current generation. A pass of either pipeline that dies tells the PR's lane `GRADER-FAILED #<n>` once per head, at its first failure. Both compare patches with lazy fetches off, after one fetch of every blob on both sides of each diff they compare, so a blobless checkout answers in seconds and a missing blob fails the pass at once.

**A grade item never sits unclaimed.** A pass that skipped because the PR moved does not answer its head: if the PR returns to that SHA, the next pass runs a new attempt. A settled pass whose head still has no grade reposts its logged records once, for the case where the store refused them while it ran. Once an item is past its deadline, if ten more minutes pass and every grader it is missing has nothing left to run, the runner posts one `GRADE-STOPPED` report to `grader-appeals`, `tooling_lane` and the PR's lane, naming why for each grader. It then records `grade-stopped` on the head, which closes the grade item and opens a verdict item for the lane on the lane's clock. Any grade posted at that head still clears it, and a new head starts over.

**Rulings check.** The `rulings` check runs `rulings.py check-pr --repo <checkout> --drive <drive> --pr <owner/repo>#<n> --head <sha> --base <base>` once per head per pass and holds no queued PR head. It fails open so a non-0/1 exit, a line naming no cause, or a `BLOCKER` line passes the head with nothing posted; `reference/owner-rulings.md` details `NAME-HIT`, `MECHANISM-BANNED`, `RULINGS-UNCITED`, and `rulings.py sweep`. `STORE-UNCOUNTED` names a head whose removed lines drop a stateful store, such as Valkey, Redis, ElastiCache, a volume claim or a `DROP TABLE`, while no body line counts the records it held before and after. Each cause it prints except `RESERVED-NO-ANSWER` sends one cci `note` warning per head and set of causes to the PR's lane, warning again only for a new cause and never once an `answer` replies to or resolves one. The pack's `rulings_at_merge` hook runs `check-pr` on `gh pr merge` in a drive session and advises with each cause, except on an incident lane's merge where it refuses with `RULINGS-REFUSED` while `check-pr --incident` names `RULINGS-UNCITED`, `RESERVED-NO-ANSWER`, or `TEST-REMOVED`.

## Merge mode, taps, and board blocks

**One land order.** An `lr.tap` record on the bus is the land order, and it is the only tap the landing pass and the desks read. The supervisor writes it within 30 seconds of the owner's record for a tap on any surface: a merge row approved, held or undone on the review screen or the board, an approval card, or a pick whose label starts with land, merge, approve, ship or hold on a card naming one PR. A record naming no head lands at the PR's head when the order is written. The Slack merge card posts its own `lr.tap`. A thread answer or a lane's hand carry of a tap has no owner record to read, so the lane holding it runs `lr landing order <PR> --record <seq>`. A tap with no order after 60 seconds posts a blocker to `meta-desk` and `owner-relay`, and is retried; nothing is held for it. `lr.tap` lives on the bus because the landing pass may run on another machine than the board.

At execution start, before the landing pass starts, the coordinator asks the owner once which merge mode the drive runs in and records the answer with `ccn answer add` using the question verbatim and label `program:<drive>`. The coordinator then writes the mode to `runner.json` as `landing.merge_mode`; the runner refuses to start without it, posts `MERGE-MODE-UNSET`, and re-reads it on every use. Under `every`, every PR waits for the owner's answer before it enqueues; under `auto`, every PR enqueues once green and approved. Under `high-risk`, the runner reads each head's changed files once by using `gh api --paginate repos/<repo>/pulls/<n>/files`, logging `FILES-READ-FAILED` and enqueueing nothing in that prefix if the read fails. Only a PR matching a glob in `landing.high_risk_paths` covering data/migrations, SQL, backfills, credentials/secrets, release pipeline, delete fences, storage formats, or infra rows waits, and every other PR enqueues once green and approved.

Every verdict is read live from the cc-present store through `cc-present outcomes` and the coordinator's `go` records when it is used; no writer, row, card, or dashboard keeps a copy. A repository owned by the runner's own GitHub account from `gh api user` lands once green under every merge mode with no merge card, though a reserved touch still asks.

**Tap rules and exemptions.** `landing.tap_rules` maps a register row key to path globs such as `{"rule/82317b0": ["go/sand/next/*", "infra/ci/src/pipelines/test/sand.ts"]}`. In every merge mode, a head whose changed files match a row's globs while `rulings.py rows` lists that row in force waits until an approval on the coordinator's board names some head of the PR. A coordinator `go` never stands in for that tap. While waiting, the runner posts one `TAP-REQUIRED` blocker per head naming the row to the PR's lane, the coordinator, and each `landing.board_lane`, or one `TAP-UNREAD` warning to the PR's lane, the coordinator, and each `landing.desk_lane` if changed files, register, or board cannot be read. A head whose newest fidelity grade matches `landing.exempt_grades` does not need a tap or a `go` in any merge mode. Those grades default to `pure port, no interaction` and `test-only or CI-only, no non-test code`, where legacy `landing.port_phrase` replaces the first and a grade saying the port touches rebuild code keeps the tap. Such a head goes straight to the merge queue once hold, fidelity, finder, build, and CI checks pass, sending one `EXEMPT-ADMITTED` note to the coordinator and each `landing.board_lane` when a tap rule names its files. Both classes and any `go` carrying `[exempt: <class>]` are verified against every file the PR changes against its base. A class is a test or CI class when it names a test, CI, `harness`, or fixture word, so `ci-tooling` is one; any other class is the coordinator's to claim. `fidelity-pipeline` reads both lists too, and its `ok` names a class the changed paths alone decide whenever every file the head changes fits it: `test-only`, `ci-only` and the default test-only grade take test and CI files, and `ci-tooling` adds the remote-builder, cc-remote and mise pins. The model never names those; it names only a class that takes judgment, such as `regression` or `owner-pick`. A head with one product file gets no path class. `lr landing go <pr>` without `--lane` is a dry pass: it prints, for the PR at its exact head, every reason the gate refuses an exempt go and posts nothing. A lane writing an exempt go runs it first and reads the class from the files, never from a grade's words or a guess.
- A test-only or CI-only claim keeps the tap on any file not recognized as a test file by `testfiles.py` or under `__tests__/`, `testdata/`, `fixtures/`, `docs/`, `.buildkite/`, `.github/`, `tools/ci/`, `infra/ci/`, `.agents/skills/*/scripts/`, `.agents/skills/*/tests/`, or buildkite yaml. It opens the `TAP-REQUIRED` blocker with `TEST-ONLY-MISMATCH` naming the first such file.
- A port claim keeps the tap on any file outside `go/sand/next/`, opening the blocker with `PORT-MISMATCH` naming the file, unless a PR comment under `## Rulings` names the interaction and cites `# owner: <answer id>`.

**Exempt go gate.** A lane posts an exempt go only through `lr landing go <pr>... --lane <lane>`, never by hand or from its own script; without `--lane` the pass is dry. It reads each PR's records once and posts only when all of these hold at the PR's current head on GitHub:

- The finder-desk's newest grade is `ok`, or a coordinator `decide` on lane `root` reads `finder-override #<n> at <sha>: <seq>` citing a record that exists.
- Every fidelity lane's newest grade is `ok`, or a coordinator `decide` reads `fidelity-override #<n> at <sha>`, and some `ok` grade names one of `landing.exempt_grades` or `landing.go_classes`, which becomes the go's class. Only the gate reads `landing.go_classes`, so a class listed there never sends a head to the queue without a go.
- No typed `hold` record holds the PR, and no open card or tap row on the owner's board names it.
- No `landing.tap_rules` glob matches a changed file, and no live go already names the head.

An override that names an owner row or answer quotes his exact words right after the key: `rule/da49cb1, "no cloud deletion at all"` or `answer 79e14c4, "Go look into it"`. Read the row whole first with `lr rules row <key>`, every repeat and implication with it; a cut read is how 13 of 55 overrides misdescribed him. The override still counts whatever the check says, and root gets an `OVERRIDE-WARNING` when a quote is not in the row, starts or stops mid clause with no `...` marking the cut, has no row id, or its row or answer could not be read. Root reads those warnings. `lr rules quotes` runs the same check on stdin before a post. It catches a wrong key or a cut, not a whole clause used for the wrong thing, so the row's words still have to say what the override says they say.

The gate reads the board's open cards and tap rows, `landing.tap_rules`, the exempt classes and `go_lanes` from the newest `landing-gate-inputs` note the Mac's supervisor posts, within a minute of a change and every five minutes otherwise, so it needs no drive registry or board daemon and runs the same on a Sprite. With no such note in 15 minutes it refuses to run rather than read the board as empty.

The same note carries the lanes, fix lanes and incident clocks the landing mover reads from the lanes machine. It lists each lane whose retirement chain ends at a lane that is not retired, and every fix lane not retired or retired in the last 14 days, each with the PRs it owns. It also names the live incident clocks and the board session a reserved row's card draws on. A supervisor whose `movers` lack `lanes` copies those lanes and their PRs into its own store every minute, so a refusal still reaches the lane that holds the PR and an incident fix still squashes. Until it has read a fresh note, it names no lane a fix. The lanes mover's checks, tick and relay run only where `lanes` is a mover, so the Mac and a landing-only Sprite never both run them.

A refused PR prints every reason and posts nothing. Text in an ask, answer, note, or blocker is never an override or a pick. A hold the coordinator writes as prose stops nothing, so it posts every hold as a `hold` record. `lr landing go <pr> --head <sha> --before <seq>` replays a past go over the records before it.

**Reserved rows.** A head whose own diff matches a reserved row's `ops` waits for the owner in every mode; a grader's `touches <key>` line alone never holds a head, and a coordinator `go` never covers a reserved row. Its `MERGE-ASK` card or board row quotes the row's key and the first sentence of its words. After an affirmative tap or pick, the coordinator runs `rulings.py authorize --drive <drive> --row <key> --action <tap ts>` with the Slack `action_ts` or board row `at`, `--block <choice block> --option <picked option>`, or `--block <approval block>`, which records an `authorizes:<key>` answer so `check-pr` stops naming the row.

**Slack merge cards (`landing.merge_ask_surface`: `slack`, default).** For each waiting PR and head, the runner posts or edits in place one merge card by using `cc-present ask --session <coordinator's newest session in drive registry>`, logging `MERGE-ASK` or `MERGE-CARD-FAILED` to retry next pass. The card carries the question in plain words, title, author, size, review state, live checks hyperlink, and buttons `Merge (Recommended)`, `Hold`, `Other`, and `Explain more` waiting on `merge:<n>:<short sha>`, never an `AskUserQuestion`, and is never hand-written by the coordinator. On a `slack.action` directive, `Merge`, `Hold`, `Other`, and `Explain more` act as follows:
- `Merge`: The coordinator posts `cci post --drive <drive> --lane root --kind go --pr <n> --text "merge #<n> at <short sha>"`. Only the opening `go` or `merge` clause followed by `#<n> at <short sha>` pairs joined by commas or `and` names a PR; prose mentions, routing notes, or a `go` answered by a coordinator `withdraw --re` merge nothing.
- `Hold`: The coordinator runs `cci post --kind hold --pr <n> --text "hold #<n> <why>"`.
- `Other`: opens a Slack modal whose text reaches the coordinator as a capture and merges nothing.
- `Explain more`: resolves nothing; the coordinator posts the `explain more` ask and replies with `cc-present reply --block <waits id> --body <answer>`.

**Board surface when `landing.merge_ask_surface` is `board`.** The runner posts no Slack card and the coordinator asks every merge question on its board. Under `every`, the runner checks only heads a coordinator `go` names and bars every other open PR like a held one. No `stack-enqueue` call names or sweeps it in, and a go'd PR stacked on it waits with one note to the coordinator per stack when its un-go'd parents change. After go'd trees enqueue, the pass's `ccx vcs pr status` read routes conflicts, red CI, and requested changes to lanes and runs accept checks on landable heads so `MERGE-ASK` reaches the board.

A coordinator `go` claiming the owner's tap on a non-exempt head without a matching board approval draws one `GO-TAP-MISSING` blocker to the coordinator. Under `every`, the merge-mode check refuses a head with `TAP-REQUIRED merge mode every` until the owner's newest board verdict on any block and at any head approves the PR. A later rejection vacates any prior approval, dequeues a queued head on the next pass, and is re-checked before a direct merge. When an open PR has an approval on a `pr-groups` row at an older head, no verdict at the current head, and no newer rejection, the runner carries the tap to the current head on every pass. It uses `cc-present verdict carry` or a `board-write` carry record, logging `TAP-CARRY-FAILED` or a recorder `defect` record on failure. The pass then posts one `TAP-CARRIED` note naming both heads to the coordinator and `landing.board_lane`, and posts its own `go` record for that head.

An owner word that approves the whole board, such as "every pending PR on the board is approved", reaches a landing path only as verdict records. The coordinator records it with `board_watch.py blanket --session <board> --word <seq> --at <time of his word> --program <drive>`, which approves each row of the board snapshot posted by that time at the head the snapshot showed. A row put up after the word waits on his tap at its own row. The runner, `mq.py gate`, `mq.py enqueue`, and a desk's own tap watch read those records, never the order that quotes the word (coordinator 313672, 314327).

**Pushing board merge-triage blocks.** Every push of a merge-triage block goes through `board_rows.py --config <runner config> <block.json> -- cc-present update-block <args>`, where `--check` runs the gate alone. The script strips `textAt` before calling `cc-present` and stops the push on any failing row `merge-row-<pr>[-<sha7>]`; a refusal goes to the coordinator and the row is fixed before the push, never pushed as it stands. It refuses a row when `commit <sha>` in the hint is not the PR's GitHub head, or when `textAt` is not after the head's commit. A row is also refused when the PR is closed, merged, superseded, or left landed in any list, or a blocker or one-tap claim sits over a landed or rejected PR. It also refuses a row missing a plain-words label or any of the `For users`, `Differs from plan`, `Risk`, `Finder`, `Build (CI)`, or `Gate` fields. Last, it refuses a row that hides a recorded verdict decided under another block or dropped id without a coordinator re-key record, or asks a tap already given or refused as `approved`, `re-ask`, or `rejected`. The gate also refuses a block that has two rows reading as one with `alike` or `FLAG` lines for matching first sentences, or has an `ungraded` or `unsettled` fidelity grade. A head lacking a `finder: ok|major|issues <sha>`, `finder [<pr>] issues <sha>`, or `finders/<pr>-<sha7>.md` review prints a `WARN` line.

## Holds, priority, dequeue, and requeue

**Holds.** Holds travel only as cci records or the GitHub `hold` label; no adapter takes a hold flag. The coordinator holds a PR with `cci post --kind hold --pr <n> --text "hold #<n> <why>"`. A record holds the one PR its text leads with as `hold #<n>`, else every PR its refs name, while a later `#<n>` in the text is prose. Only a typed `--resolves` on a hold lifts it; a `go` never does. Each pass reads open holds, and a queued head under an open hold leaves the queue top first with every PR stacked on it and a `DEQUEUED` record per PR. A head the owner taps under an open hold draws one `DECIDE` to the coordinator per head and hold, naming the hold's record; the hold stands until the coordinator lifts it. A lane whose stack sits on a held PR reads why with `lr board waits --pr <n>` and leaves release to the coordinator.

**Priority PRs.** When the owner flags a PR as priority or it blocks a release or user, the coordinator runs `lr board waits --pr <n>` in the same turn under D3 and routes whatever it names. Priority approval covers only the head the owner named; if the PR gains commits or scope, the coordinator gets fresh approval naming the new head. Never relay an ETA for a green priority PR.

**Dequeue and hand enqueue.** To take a PR out of the queue, the coordinator posts a `hold` on it: the next pass pulls it and every PR above it and posts `DEQUEUED`, or `DEQUEUE-REFUSED` with what the queue said. Resolving the hold lets it enter again. A queue cancel after a pull reads as that pull, never as an eviction.

A hand `stack-enqueue --dequeue` records nothing and draws a warning naming `cci post --kind hold`. A hand `stack-enqueue` runs `lr landing gate <n>...` by using the `lane_landing` hook and warns on failing checks, so never wrap `stack-enqueue` in a custom script. In a Forge-AI/monorepo drive, every hand landing, whether `gh pr merge`, `ccx vcs merge`, `gh stack merge`, `stack-enqueue`, `ci trunk submit`, or a `merge` label, is blocked when that gate prints `TAP-REQUIRED`, the same tap read the runner makes, so a carried or standing tap counts. The refusal names the PR and head and points to a card asking the owner's tap, or to the runner. `desk-runner`, `merge-train`, and desk landings, an incident lane's plain squash, and merges in other repos such as yasyf/* are not hand landings. A gate that does not read warns, names the failure, and lets the landing through.

## Five-minute go-to-queue and overflow

A green PR a coordinator `go` names at its head enters the merge queue within five minutes of that `go`. A stack left out that long, unheld and unrefused, goes to a stack worker through the runner's overflow. Overflow runs the same check, admission, and merge-mode gates as a pass and sends the whole stack once any PR in it is due, and a blocked enqueue five minutes old no longer keeps it out. Every head a `go` or exemption admits gets, from the pass that admits it, one `QUEUED`, `REFUSED`, or `BELOW-BLOCKED` record per change of outcome; `REFUSED` names the gate line or check, `REFUSED unsettled:` with the last branch if unsettled, or `gating off: <why>` when gating is off. A go'd head whose queue draft closed unmerged is dequeued once and enqueued afresh as `ORPHAN-RELEASED`, never `--recover`ed.

The runner logs one `GO-UNQUEUED` per head still out only when its own newest enqueue of that head since the `go` ended blocked, failed, stranded, or unsettled, or was evicted after enqueue, never for backlog. That log names the outcome or eviction time and reason, and the coordinator clears that blocker in the same turn or spawns a lane to do so. A head evicted before any enqueue gets one blocker to the coordinator and its lane. A green admitted head with no `ai-review` run on a PR the review bot reviewed at an earlier head gets one `@forge-pr-reviewer review` comment and one `REVIEW-REREQUESTED` note once per head. When the bot's newest `ai-review` run reads `Held`, the line states the bot holds it on N blocking findings and routes one record per head to the owning lane to fix or accept them. `Waiting for restack` reads as waiting for a restack push, and neither is a missing verdict.

## Graphite down and direct merge

On `trunk-stack-enqueue`, Trunk failures or unreadable queue states stay `BLOCKED` or `UNKNOWN` and never trigger direct merges or automated orphan/own-red/flake recovery; native retry is a manual `stack-enqueue --flake` after inspecting verification.

On `graphite-stack-enqueue` only, Graphite reads down when no batch has started in 15 minutes while PRs are queued, or its two newest batches both timed out. The runner posts `GRAPHITE-DOWN` to the coordinator and the landing desks, stops overflow, and becomes the sole merger with one merge in flight at a time, squash-merging each green, approved, unheld stack it would have enqueued, including evicted heads. If a stack is behind `dev`, the runner rebases it with `git replay` and keeps it only when its own patch is unchanged. That pass pushes in one atomic push leased on the admitted heads and waits up to 10 minutes for the review bot's re-approval. Any conflict, changed patch, or missing approval routes as one blocker per head to the PR's lane and `landing.restack_lane`. A lone PR merges with `gh pr merge --squash --match-head-commit` and a longer chain with `gh stack link` and one all-or-nothing `gh stack merge --squash`, waiting for each squash and posting `MERGED #N at <head> direct as <squash>, Graphite down, b530f5d`. It posts `GRAPHITE-UP` after the first batch Graphite starts again.

## Trunk health and redundant skips

**Trunk health (`landing.trunk_check`).** With `"trunk_check": "buildkite/test"` in `landing`, every pass reads that status over the trunk's last 50 commits. A red run failing a job that a PR build also runs posts a `DEV-RED for <n> min` incident to the coordinator and `drive-watchdog` on topic `dev-red`, naming the jobs, first red commit, and landed PR, and stating no PR can land until fixed. It repeats every 30 minutes while red, then posts `DEV-GREEN` once when it passes; the fix lane names its PR with `cci post --topic dev-red --pr <n>`. The runner holds no row because required checks hold what cannot land. Meanwhile, `board_rows.py` keeps every tap row on the board and prefixes merge card prompts with `dev's test build is red since <time>; nothing lands until #<fix> fixes it.`, and the pack refuses any `"READY"` report or coordinator ruling calling a head `"green except"` a job, a job `"known red"`, or a trunk `"red by design"`. When a red run fails only jobs no PR build runs, or a newer finished trunk build read by using `landing.ci_build` fails or soft-fails a job more often than in the build before it, the runner posts `POST-MERGE-RED`. That post goes to the coordinator, `landing.desk_lane`, and the lanes of PRs landed between the two builds, while landing continues and a fix is owed.

`landing.ci_token_command` is the argv that prints this host's read-only Buildkite token, the same command `bk.json` names as `token_command`. The supervisor hands its value as `BUILDKITE_TOKEN` to the trunk watch's `ci_build` and landing-build reads, the release-build watch's `buildkite_cli`, the page watch's `pages.build`, the build-failure gate's `build_failures.annotations`, and `release_cli status` and `release_cli live`. A write, such as an incident's rebuild, keeps its own credentials. No read uses a person's `bk auth login`, since that OAuth token lasts one hour and `bk auth token` prints it without renewing it.

Each reader runs the command at its first read and keeps the value in memory until Buildkite answers 401, then runs it again at its next read; the trunk watch also runs it once per pass. The value is never written to a file or a record. Without the setting those reads run nothing and their log line or escalation names it, and a 401 reads as `auth failure: Buildkite refused the read token landing.ci_token_command printed`.

**Flakes (`landing.flake_lane`).** A trunk job that fails and passes on retry, or a flaky notice in `landing.flaky_channel`, posts one `FLAKE` defect naming the failing test, or the job when no test failed. A job whose log shows a GitHub quota or rate-limit refusal and no failing test posts `INFRA` instead, so it is never called a flaky test. The defect goes to the live lane of the `flake_lane` stem. With none live, the supervisor writes the stem's standing brief to `<state>/<stem>/brief.md` and launches the next number from it, and root gets one `note` it need not act on. The fix is due in two hours. Each pass, an open record with no live lane on it gets the next lane of the stem, handed over in a `note`. A repeat failure of the same test or job attaches to the open record as a `note`. A resolver that names no merged PR reopens the record to the same owner. An open record past its due time, or with no live lane on it, becomes an ask in the program ledger, so it shows in the owner update and on the Your asks page. Automatic retries stay, and a retry that turns the build green never closes the flake.

A failed Buildkite read does not silence the trunk. `DEV-RED` and `DEV-GREEN` come from GitHub's statuses and still post, and a red whose jobs cannot be read reads as `DEV-RED`, since nothing proves its jobs run only after a merge. Job comparisons and flake reports wait for a pass that can read the build. A 401 escalates as `not a red build`, followed by the auth failure. A failed GitHub history read posts nothing that pass. After any background pass that escalated succeeds, the supervisor posts one `<pass> recovered` note to the lane that heard the failure.

**Merges past their own red (`merge_own_red`).** In every session, drive or not, the pack blocks `gh pr merge`, with or without `--admin`, `gh stack merge`, and `ccx vcs merge` when a finished failing check names a file that a PR the merge lands changes. `gh pr merge` lands one PR. `ccx vcs merge` lands its PR and every open PR below it in the native stack, and so does `gh stack merge` given a PR; given a stack number or nothing, it lands the whole stack. `lr own-red` makes that decision for every path by reading each check's annotations, or its Actions job log when it annotates no file, and the block lists each PR, check, `file:line`, and finding. A red check whose findings all sit outside its PR's diff is inherited from the base branch, so the merge runs with a note naming it, and a pending check never blocks. In a lane's own window, the same `lr own-red` call first reads the lane's records through its cursor, `cci tail --cursor <lane> --reader <lane>`, and blocks while one another lane addressed to it has no closing reply, no resolving record, and no answer from the lane. The lane's own records never count. The read is never paged, so one block lists every such record, then the oldest and newest 20 of the rest with a line naming how to read those between, and the next attempt has read them. A window with no lane binding reads nothing.

**Redundant skips (`landing.ci_build` and `landing.ci_grade`).** With `"ci_build": "tools/ci bk api /pipelines/{pipeline}/builds/{number}"`, the runner reads each passing Buildkite build at a green head once. A head whose only passing build is Graphite's `"Redundant within its stack"` skip is graded `unproven: redundant skip, child #<n> <state>`, sent to the lane as a blocker, and kept out of merge cards and enqueues along with every PR above it. It waits until a full build of that pipeline passes on a commit containing the head, either at the stack's tip or named by the lane in a `full run green <build>: #<pr> #<pr>` note or report. A failed build at the head itself always wins as `unproven: its own build <url> failed`. With `"ci_grade": "tools/ci bk api -X POST -d '{\"commit\":\"{commit}\",\"branch\":\"{branch}\",\"ignore_pipeline_branch_filters\":true}' /pipelines/{pipeline}/builds"` also set, when no tip build covers or is running for the head, the runner points branch `ci-grade/<pr>` at the head and runs `ci_grade` once per head, logging `CI-GRADE-FAILED` to retry next pass on failure; the head reads `unproven: redundant skip, ..., so full build <url> grades <sha>` until that build passes.

## Incident fixes and release on landing

**Incident fix merges.** Incident fixes land by plain squash through `gh pr merge <n> --squash --match-head-commit <head>`, taking the same rebase as a Graphite-down merge when behind `dev`. They merge in the pass their head is approved and green, under every merge mode and even during `checks_wait_for_go`, and never enter the trunk queue or use `--fast`. The fidelity desks grade an incident fix row first in their queue and never waive the plan check, while the finder grade does not gate the merge and logs `UNGRADED-30` once if no `finder: ok` arrives within 30 minutes after landing. `check-pr --incident` refuses the merge with one cci `RULINGS-REFUSED` blocker to the PR's lane and the coordinator if it names `RULINGS-UNCITED`, `RESERVED-NO-ANSWER`, or `TEST-REMOVED`, merging in the first pass they clear. Just before `gh pr merge`, the runner reads open cci `hold` records again; if no open incident record names the fix or its lane, a hold refuses it. While an open incident record names the fix or its lane, a hold logs `INCIDENT-HOLD-WARNED` once, the fix merges anyway, and the incident clock launches no Opus fallback.

**Release on landing (D20).** `landing.release_owner` in `runner.json`, a cci `--to` address such as `peer:<session>`, is required for the landing pass to start. The runner judges each landing once; on its first pass after startup or upgrade it records existing landings as history, waits until `landed_at` is set, and closes pre-start landings silently when `tools/ci release live --json` already shows the squash or a later commit live. A `merge-fast` landing whose body has no `Release-With:` line is read once and sends nothing. An incident fix owes components from its `Release-With:` lines or else the release its squash needs whatever its label. When any incident fix lands, or when a row lands with the `merge-fast` label and a `Release-With:` line in its body read by using `gh pr view <pr> --json body`, the runner posts:
`cci post --drive <drive> --lane desk-runner --kind release --to <landing.release_owner>,root --topic <pr> --pr <pr> --id release:<pr>:<step> --text "<squash, components, start the release now>"`

A post cci refuses or that prints no sequence logs `RELEASE-FAILED` once per step and retries next pass. The release owner starts the release and posts `cci post --drive <drive> --lane <lane> --kind release --pr <pr> --target <component> --build <build url> --text <release id>` on this drive; `reference/release-lanes.md` covers runner claims, announcements, and pre/post-cut `pup` and `endstate.py` checks. Until settled, the runner posts one `blocker` to `<landing.release_owner>,root` every `deadlines.release_start_minutes`, which defaults to `10`, or every `deadlines.incident_release_minutes`, which defaults to `3`, while an incident clock runs. A landing first read after a step has passed gets the `release` record then and its first blocker at the next step.
- A `merge-fast` landing settles on a `release` record on this drive stamped at or after landing from any lane but `desk-runner` naming a component with `--target` or the PR with `--pr`, a `fix-live` record from another lane naming the PR or component, or a coordinator `--re <seq>` or `--resolves <seq>` on the release record or a blocker.
- An incident landing settles only on a `release` record carrying both `--build` and `--target <component>`, a `fix-live` record from another lane, or a coordinator `--resolves` naming the release record, a blocker, or `RELEASE-HELD-AFTER-LANDING`, while `--re` does not settle it. An open coordinator `hold` record naming a landed incident PR or addressed to the release owner draws one `RELEASE-HELD-AFTER-LANDING #<pr>: landed <sha> at <time>; its cut is owed now, the other PR gets its own cut when it lands` line while blockers keep their cadence. Only a `hold` record is a hold; a `decide`, `decision`, or `go` that mentions a PR or says `"wait"` is not, and a coordinator `--resolves` on the hold ends it.

Once the settling record names `--build`, each pass reads `tools/ci release live <id> --json` and posts one `RELEASED <component> <version> (<build>)` record with `--kind fix-live --target <component> --build <url>` and key `release:<pr>:live` to the coordinator alone when every component reads live; it is the only publish line the coordinator forwards to the owner.

**Cut clocks (`CUT-SILENT`).** A cut order is a coordinator `go` or `decision` addressed to a release lane carrying `--target <component>` whose text orders a cut or `release start`. A D20 `release` record with no build yet whose landing names `Release-With:` components is also a cut order. Prose mentions that omit `--target` do not open a clock. The release lane answers an order that `--resolves` its own `release --build` or `refuse` record. The cut clock stays open until landing settlement, the coordinator `--resolves`, or another lane's `release` or `fix-live` record closes it. If the release lane posts no `release --build` or `refuse` record within `deadlines.cut_minutes`, which defaults to `4`, while the clock is open, the orca runner posts `CUT-SILENT <lane> <component>: no CUT or refusal <n> min after GO #<seq>; hand path: tools/ci release start --manual <component> --env <env> --commit <sha> --emergency` to the coordinator once. That post claims nothing, and `--emergency` only skips red dev checks. After a 1-minute wait, the runner queues `manual-release-<component>` on opus xhigh from `reference/manual-release-brief.md`, withdrawing the launch as `WITHDRAWN cut:<key> manual-release-<component>: <why>; nothing was launched` if resolved or answered during that minute, and relaying any later `already ships <component> at <sha> or later` refusal to that lane.

## Desk cadence and commands

Create the ledger once at spawn and put its id in every brief. A resumed coordinator in a new session runs `drive.py start --drive <id> --ledger <id>` to join the existing drive, and runs `drive.py end` only when the drive is over. `--register-lane <lane>` names the lane that classifies owner captures; while listed in `cci who`, it receives the register gate's unclassified-capture blocker and holds the `classify`, `names`, `authorize`, and `supersede` seats, and otherwise, the blocker goes to the coordinator, which resumes the lane by `SendMessage`.

```sh
LEDGER=$(ledger.py init --title "desk: $DRIVE")
drive.py start --ledger "$LEDGER" [--orca-run <run>] [--state-dir ~/.claude/scratch/<slug>] [--program <plan slug>] [--register-lane <lane>]
drive.py desk --name <desk lane> --terminal <handle> [--environment <name>] [--supervisor]
ledger.py ask     --ledger "$LEDGER" --text "<verbatim>" --lane lightning-eh --accept "<acceptance check>"

# on each report: record it and grade the current head; reports are not a gate
ledger.py report  --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --pr 21221 --head <sha> --lane lightning-eh --verdict clean --ask ask/000001
ledger.py report  --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --repo yasyf/cc-skills --pr 375 --head <sha> --lane skills-tool --verdict clean
ledger.py ruling  --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --lane p2-edge-rows --pr 20284 --text "land without the document form" --options "A land|B hold|C close"
ledger.py enqueue --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --kind idle --pr 21221 --head <sha> --lane lightning-eh --text "done"
ledger.py inbox   --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --take
ledger.py register --ledger "$LEDGER" --lane lightning-eh --branch-prefix lightning/ --pr 21221

# every 3 minutes, staggered :00/:01/:02: reconcile every tracked current head
ledger.py refresh --ledger "$LEDGER"
ledger.py reconcile --repo "$REPO" --ledger "$LEDGER" --checkout "$CHECKOUT"
ledger.py route   --repo "$REPO" --ledger "$LEDGER"
ledger.py stale   --ledger "$LEDGER"

ledger.py route --repo "$REPO" --ledger "$LEDGER" --pr 21221 --job "plan comment missing for this head"
ledger.py hold  --ledger "$LEDGER" --pr 20284 --reason "waits on #20314" --hours 4
ledger.py hold  --ledger "$LEDGER" --pr 20290 --reason "argo cutover waits on the owner" --hours 12 --stack
ledger.py hold  --ledger "$LEDGER" --pr 21052 --reason "dev-red:infra-plan-observability" --hours 6

# hot-set conflicts and a gone lane's hot-set rows go to the train, its other rows to the red desk
ledger.py gone  --ledger "$LEDGER" --lane lightning-eh
ledger.py route --repo "$REPO" --ledger "$LEDGER" --train merge-train --paths 'infra/ci/src/pipelines/release/**' 'infra/engine.ts' --fallback red-desk

ledger.py summary --repo "$REPO" --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --checkout "$CHECKOUT"
ledger.py show    --ledger "$LEDGER" --asks
ledger.py live    --ledger "$LEDGER" --at "$(date -u +%FT%TZ)" --text "release 37 deployed"
ledger.py drop    --ledger "$LEDGER" --ask ask/000002 --reason "owner withdrew it"
ledger.py answer  --ledger "$LEDGER" --ask ask/000003 --text "<the reply>"

lr board waits --pr 21221
lr landing gate 21221
cci post --kind hold --pr 21221 --text "hold #21221 the owner pulled it"

drive.py end
```

At spawn, arm the watch under Monitor at its maximum timeout and re-arm on every expiry:

```sh
ledger.py watch --repo "$REPO" --ledger "$LEDGER" --drive "$DRIVE" --checkout "$CHECKOUT" [--priority <n>]... [--shard <lane>,<lane>]
```

| Verb / flag | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `ledger.py watch` | Re-reads non-terminal PR rows each pass, records transitions, settles landings/closures by using `landed`, writes a P0 inbox message, and prints `P0 #<n> <event> <lane>` for every ejection and for `conflicting` or `red` on a priority, desk-labeled/enqueued, or previously queued row. A PR that had no entry in the snapshot when a pass first saw it and already landed or closed emits nothing and settles nothing because `landed` settles it, while a standing `conflicting`, `red`, or `ejected` still emits once. `--shard` keeps its own snapshot at `~/.cache/ccn-ledger/<ledger>.<lanes>.watch.json`. |
| `ledger.py label` | Used where the repo has no enqueue script, taking `--pr <tip> --expect-head <sha> --checkout <path>` or `--all-clean` for the sequential fallback sweep. Records `label_refused`, `label_refused_head`, and `label_refused_at` on refused stacks and clears them on success. Run `label --dry-run` once on a repo before the first live label. `unlabel --reason` records why a label came off and blocks re-labeling that head. |
| `ledger.py stale` | `--minutes N` sets the clean-report age threshold, which defaults to `30`. Also names every open PR opened at least `--hours` ago, defaulting to `60`, as `aged #N <h>h <lane>: <state>`; land or close it before the 72-hour full re-review applies. |
| `ledger.py route` | Without `--pr`, sweeps every red or conflicting row, reads the first failing line from the Buildkite log, prints the message to send, and records it, while `--dry-run` prints only. With `--train <lane> --paths <globs> --fallback <lane>`, conflicting rows matching `<globs>` and any matching row of a `gone` lane go to `<train>`, a `gone` lane's other rows go to `<fallback>`, and a live lane keeps its own reds. Skips rows held `dev-red:<step>` until expiry. |
| `ledger.py show` | `--asks` prints each ask with its computed state, or `[pending]` while it is too fresh to grade; `--red` answers a lane asking what it owns, and the desk never sends the raw table. |

## Message contracts

### The lane report, three lines

A lane sends this when it ships a PR, pushes a new head, or reaches a terminal state on one, while registration goes separately:

```
PR #<n> <head sha, full> <clean|red|conflicting|held>
<what changed on this head, one line>
<what is next, one line, or "none">
```

For an owner ask, append an optional fourth line, `ask <id>`, such as `ask/000104` or `000104`. A lane with the ledger id and cci drive records the report itself with `ledger.py report --ledger <id> --drive <drive> --repo <owner/name> --pr <n> --head <sha> --lane <name> --verdict <v> --text "<line 2>" [--ask <id>]`, where `"READY"` is `--verdict clean` and an unknown ask id exits non-zero. A PR row's key is `<owner/repo>#<n>`, defaulting `--repo` to the ledger checkout's repository. Duplicate reports with the same PR, head, and verdict are dropped. Reporting lanes follow four rules:
- Pass the full pushed head sha, and verify `git ls-remote origin refs/heads/<branch>` matches local `HEAD` before reporting so a detached worktree does not silently push nothing.
- Re-report every new push in the same turn, and check `ccx vcs pr status <n>` before pushing to a reported PR. If it is `queued`, ship the delta as a new stacked PR instead of amending a branch the queue already snapshotted. After the `merge` label goes on, the branch is not the lane's to amend and further work is a new branch off a fresh trunk.
- Report `conflicting` while mid-rebase and push nothing labeled; report `clean` again only on the rebased head. Put prose in a cc-notes note cited on line 2.
- Running `ccx vcs ship` records each PR it opens by using the hook and posts `opened` on cci in cci 0.19.0 or later, carrying the raw PR body and the repo, branch, base, head and parent attrs the review row folds; a later push posts `head` the same way, with the body when the call changes it. A drive lane's ship that would open a PR with no `--pr-body-file` is refused before it runs. A Codex worker gets neither from `ship` and runs `drive.py record --opened` after it.

### Owner asks in the ledger

The coordinator records every owner ask in the turn it arrives per R8 in `SKILL.md` by running `ledger.py ask --ledger <id> --text "<verbatim>" --lane <lane> --accept "<acceptance check>" [--standing [HOURS]]`, which prints `ask/000001 <lane>: <text>`. Lanes record their own sub-dispatches the same way with `ledger.py ask` before dispatch and `ledger.py answer` when the reply lands. A PR carrying an `IN-PR` ask takes no distinct second ask; `report --ask` refuses it, so put the new ask on a stacked follow-up PR.

| Ask status | Meaning and transition |
| --- | --- |
| `LIVE` | Every linked PR has landed and `ledger.py live --ledger <id> --at <ISO>` recorded the shipping pipeline running at or after the latest landing, or the ask was pinned with `ledger.py ask --state live`; a per-ask state outranks the ledger-wide `live --at`. Only `LIVE` counts as done; before then, the coordinator says `"in #N, not live yet: <blocker>"`. |
| `LANDED-NOT-LIVE` | Every linked PR has landed, awaiting `ledger.py live`. |
| `IN-PR` | At least one linked PR is still open. Escalated in the summary once older than 60 minutes. |
| `IN-PROGRESS` | No linked PR, but `asked_at` or the latest `ledger.py progress --ledger <id> --ask <id> --note "<what moved>" [--artifact <url or id>]...` entry is under 30 minutes old, or under `HOURS`, which defaults to `24`, for a `--standing` ask. |
| `LOST` | No open or landed PR and at least 30 minutes past `asked_at` or the latest `progress` entry, or pinned with `--state lost`. Dispatched by the coordinator in the same turn. |
| `STALE` | A `--standing` ask whose `asked_at` and latest `progress` entry are older than `HOURS`. |
| `BACKLOG` | Marked with `ledger.py ask --ledger <id> --state backlog --ask <id> --note "<where it went>"`, while `--state open` restores computed state. |
| `dropped` / `answered` | Terminal states set by `ledger.py drop --ledger <id> --ask <id> --reason "<why>"` when withdrawn or `ledger.py answer --ledger <id> --ask <id> --text "<reply>"` on a question or sub-dispatch. |

### One-line `RULING NEEDED` format

Recorded by `ledger.py ruling --drive <drive> --text "<question>" --options "A <..>|B <..>|C <..>"`, where two rulings with the same question on the same PR are one ruling. The desk forwards it immediately to the coordinator, which answers with the letter alone and never replies to an idle notice:

```
RULING NEEDED: <the question, one line>; options: A <..> / B <..> / C <..>
```

### Routing, desk to lane

Printed by `ledger.py route` and sent once per PR, head, and failing job:

```
DESK #<n> <head9>: <failing job, or "rebase onto <base>">
<the first failing line from the Buildkite log, when the desk read one>
Push the fix as a new head on the same branch, then send landing-desk the 3-line report
(PR, head, verdict); the merge label waits on that head reading green and merge-clean.
```

### The desk to coordinator summary, every 30 minutes

`ledger.py summary --drive <drive>` runs `reconcile` over every non-terminal row in the ledger first, then prints the summary. `merged/h` counts squash commits on the trunk, the repo's default branch, inside the window, read from the git log by `ledger.py landed`, which fetches the trunk and settles closed rows by tree equality and then a squash subject ending `(#n)`. The `p50` is the median minutes from each landed row's last report to its landing over the window, or `-` when nothing landed. The desk sends the summary unchanged, preceded by any non-clear lines from `lr watch prompts show` as `reference/watchers.md` describes, and followed by `lr handoff standing --drive <drive>` output per I6 in `SKILL.md` and `reference/owner-rulings.md` plus `cursor #<seq>`.

```
desk <stamp> | open N | merged/h N | labelled N | held N | rulings N | p0 N | routed N | stale N | p50 report→landed Nm | lost N | landed-not-live N
LOST ask/<n> <lane>: <text>
STALE ask/<n> <lane> standing 24h: <text> | progress 1500m: <note> <artifact>
LANDED-NOT-LIVE ask/<n> <lane>: <text>
IN-PROGRESS ask/<n> <lane>: <text> | progress 12m: <note> <artifact>
IN-PR 61m ask/<n> <lane>: <text>: #<pr> <blocker>
stale #i 47m <lane>: <blocker>
waiting: ungraded #a | refused #b | red #c | held #d
merged: #a #b
labelled: #c #d
P0 #e <lane>: <text>
RULING NEEDED: <question>; options: A / B / C
held #f: <reason> until <stamp>[ EXPIRED]
routed, awaiting a new head: #g #h
```

Prompt-watch lines preceding the ask lines take this shape:

```text
WAITING-ON-PROMPT <name> <role> approval|question <n>m terminal=<handle>: screen saved at <path>[; supervisor <state>]
PROMPT-STALE <name> <role> <n>m terminal=<handle>: <detail>
PROMPT-UNKNOWN <name> <role> <n>m terminal=<handle>: <detail>
PROMPT-UNREACHABLE <name> <role> <n>m terminal=<handle> environment=<name>: <detail>
PROMPT-WATCH-DOWN <drive>: <detail>; start it with `<command>`
```

Submit any worker-directed ruling from prompt watch with `lr relay <lane> <text>`, and never send it to an orca-desk lane. Ask lines sit outside the ten-line cap on desk lines; when more desk lines remain, the ten-line block ends with a pointer to `ledger.py show`. Beside the summary, the coordinator reads `cci digest --drive <drive>` over its default 24-hour window, widened with `--since`, and `cci state --drive <drive>`. An ask older than its lane's cadence is dispatched under R6, a blocker on a finished lane's PR gets a fresh fix lane, and the coordinator reads `cci state --drive <drive>` before asking for a head or contract. When the 30-minute summary arrives, the coordinator updates its task list and sends nothing back.
