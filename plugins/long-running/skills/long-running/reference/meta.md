# The meta session

`drive manual meta <word>` prints the rows whose first cell names that word: read the one row a record or result names, not the whole page.

The meta session is the drive's third session, beside the communicator he talks to and the coordinator that runs his orders, and every drive has one: `drive start` opens it in an Orca terminal of its own while none is bound, as the `start` page says. It holds drive tooling, how the drive itself works: a tool it runs on (the drive CLI and its MCP tools, a hook, the watch, the dashboard, cards and posts, cc-slack check-post, the drive's CI plumbing), this skill, its agents and role rules, how its sessions reach each other and him, and the install of a released tool, so neither of the other two starts a tooling reader, designer or builder, every record about the drive reaches it alone, and the communicator's context stays small. His rulings of Nov 11, 2026: "A third session, a tooling/meta coordinator, owns every tool fix, skill rewrite and install; your session stays thin, with one more session to keep", and of Nov 12: "have the skill always start a meta root as a sibling to coordinator and communicator, and all meta stuff gets routed there, no exceptions". It runs on Opus 5.5 and reads `meta@<drive>`.

## What it owns

- Every fix to a tool the drive runs on: a hook, the `drive` CLI and its MCP tools, the watch, the dashboard, cards and the library that draws posts and cards (`lib/drive/card.py`, `picture.py`, `site/visuals.js`), `cc-slack check-post`, and CI plumbing. Each folds a new check, step, hook, flag or verb into what exists, or deletes one, before adding one, by his words of Nov 12, 2:59 AM: "in geernal keeps implifying all this tuff".
- Every rewrite of this skill, its pages, its agents and the role rules a ruling of his asks for. His words: "toolingh/meta improvements dont go to coorfidnatror".
- Every install of a released tool, through the builder that lands it.
- Every `Drive tooling:` order and every order a brief serving drive tooling gave it. `drive order add` gives it each `Drive tooling:` order, whichever session adds it, and keeps his thread on it, so his later words there find it; `drive job brief <j> --serves "drive tooling"` gives it the coordinator's order that job is on.

## What it never does

- It posts nothing to Slack, and the `message_limits` hook refuses its posts. What he should see is a `report` record to `communicator@<drive>` opening `POST`, naming what its post finishes: `--attr close=<o>` on the one that finishes a tooling order, which its handler then closes whether or not the order has a thread, `--attr part=<o>:<part>` on one delivering a part, or `--attr progress=true` on a line that finishes nothing. A question for him is an `ask` record there. The communicator's handler posts both, since his reply under a post reaches only the session that posted it.
- It touches no product order. `drive order add` refuses it an order whose words do not open `Drive tooling:`, and `drive job add` refuses it an order the coordinator holds.
- It does no order's work itself. The `landing_guard` hook refuses its own build, ship, commit or push, as it refuses the coordinator's, so each is a job's.
- It starts no handler: every handler is the communicator's.

## Start a reader

- Each tooling order is a build, so its builder starts only on its reader's research: adding the reader records its builder waiting on it, its id printed beside the reader's, started on its `FREE` line. Any other builder on a build order starts its agent only on a brief whose `Researched:` line names a reader of its part that kept the block, the one check of that research, made when its agent starts; a builder proving a merged PR live, retrying its proof or rebasing it starts on a `Handoff:` brief, which needs no reader, and `drive job add` adds it like any other.
- The order's words reach him verbatim, quoted on the line above its PR card and as the title an `{order:}` link shows. So `drive order add` refuses words `cc-slack check-post` finds anything in, such as a job, order or record id or a PR by its bare number: say what the tool does wrong in plain words. `drive order set <o> --words "Drive tooling: <what>"` rewords an older tooling order the same check refuses.
- Start a `long-running:reader` through the Agent tool with `run_in_background: true`, a short `description`, no `name`, and this prompt:

```
Order: Drive tooling: <what it fixes or changes, one line>
From: cci:<the record's seq>
Ruling (his words): <a ruling or his feedback on a post only: verbatim, with its time and permalink>
Research: the Researched: block this build needs
Native: <where he sees or uses the result, such as a Slack post or card, the dashboard or a CLI he runs, and what that platform already does for it that you know, with links, such as Block Kit's chart and table blocks; "none" when he never sees it>
Decisions that bind: <his earlier words on the area that you already hold, verbatim with their times, always with "in geernal keeps implifying all this tuff" (Nov 12, 2:59 AM)>
Would he ask: <what he would still have to ask for if only his words were built>
Drive: <name>, order <o>, job <j>
Scratch: <its scratch folder>
```

- Its `Drive` line records the agent on the job, so the `job` call needs no `agent` field.
- Write `Native`, `Decisions that bind` and `Would he ask` from what the record and his answers already hold. His words taken alone as the whole spec get only his literal words built, such as a chart drawn as a picture though Slack has its own chart block.
- The `agent_brief` hook refuses a builder whose brief has no `Serves:` or `Handoff:` line, or a `Serves:` line naming none of a port of #N, drive tooling or his order, and refuses a builder serving drive tooling from any session but yours.
- A builder cannot start agents. When a rewrite needs a check by a fresh agent on a real record, start that check yourself once the rewrite is installed.
