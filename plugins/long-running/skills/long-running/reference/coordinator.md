# The coordinator

`drive manual coordinator <word>` prints the rows whose first cell names that word: read the one row a line names, not the whole page.

## Drive verbs as tools

- Run the `drive order`, `drive job` and `drive pr` verbs that add or set, and `drive status`, as tools of this plugin's `drive` MCP server, never in Bash: `order` (`add`, `set`, `posted`), `job` (`add`, `set`, `brief`), `pr` (`add`, `set`), `status` and `search_plan`.
- One call of a tool carries every op the turn needs from it, in order, each op one verb with its flags as fields named as in `drive manual verbs`, dashes as underscores, such as `pr` with ops `[{set: {url: "<url>", serves: "o4", design: "none"}}, {set: {url: "<url 2>", waits: ["<card>"]}}]`.
- An op after an `add` that names no `id` acts on what that add made, and a `job` `add` whose `order` holds `order add`'s fields adds the order too.
- A call prints one line per op and stops at the first refusal, naming it and the ops left unrun; `dry_run: true` prints the commands and runs nothing.
- The rows below name each verb as its `drive` command. `drive answer`, `drive card`, `drive job ask`, `report`, `replies`, `launch`, `resume` and `end`, `drive place` and `drive manual` stay in Bash.
- Send an `owner` or `defect` record with the `record` tool, and a `report`, `ready` or `ask` with the cci `post` tool, since its `record_lines` hook checks them and `cci post` in Bash skips it.

## The top and each sub-coordinator

- A drive can run one sub-coordinator per project beside the top coordinator, each a session of its own bound with `drive start <drive> --role coordinator --project <project>` (the `start` page). It holds the coordinator role, so every rule and row on this page and every hook that checks that role binds it, for its project's orders.
- A sub-coordinator owns the orders filed under its project, the jobs serving or carrying them and the PRs serving them, and lands and installs its project's changes. Its own watch, `drive watch --project <project>`, hears only those, its GitHub and release reads, lane mail, ticks, promised times and `ORPHAN` lines included, and its lines arrive at `<lane>@<drive>`, `<lane>` being the project's name lowercased with each run of other characters a dash. It posts with `--lane <lane>`: its `report`, `ready`, `ask` and `defect` records go to `communicator@<drive>` directly, and his answers to its cards come back to its lane.
- The top hears orders filed under no project or under one with no sub-coordinator yet, every Slack channel the drive holds with the alerts and releases they carry, and the `ANSWER`, `EVENT catch-up` and `EVENT unheard` lines; its watch alone draws the status page and compacts the communicator. It alone starts, approves and rolls back production cuts, one at a time, since one cut carries every project's changes. His words: "the top starts, approves and rolls back production cuts, one at a time".
- The `landing_guard` hook refuses `ci release start`, `approve` and `rollback` from a sub-coordinator's session and the agents it starts, naming the top's session; `--plan`, `--dry-run`, `status` and `watch` still run. When a change of its project needs a cut his order or pick covers, the sub-coordinator sends a `release` record to `root@<drive>`, a kind the top's subscription wakes on, naming the merged changes the cut must carry, and a job of the top's starts it by [After merge](landing.md#after-merge).
- An event is stamped as it is written with the lane hearing its order then, so lines from before a binding stay the top's. An agent the top started stays the top's to `SendMessage` while its lines go to the sub-coordinator, which sends the top a `handoff` record at `root@<drive>` with what that agent must hear.
- When an `owner` record carries an order that belongs under a sub-coordinator's project, the top sends the record on with the `record` tool's `to: "<lane>"` and adds no order or job for it, since that order's lines would reach only the sub-coordinator. `drive status` lists each sub-coordinator with its project, lane, session, Orca place and whether its watch runs; the `record` tool refuses a lane no session reads, naming those that are.

## Before you act

1. When a line names an order, job or PR, read only those with `drive status <id>`, and the whole `drive status` only after a start, a restart or a compaction, since rereading repeats your context. A report calling a job stuck or silent is refused without a read of its transcript (`drive job replies <j>` or `cc-transcript`) in 10 minutes.
2. Before a report, a brief or a trigger says a PR carries a change, read its title and files with `gh pr view <url> --json title,files`, since a goal line or branch name does not show what it changes. A trigger that gates a release, a freeze or an unfreeze names the change and its branch.
3. An `owner` record carries only his words: his message, words he typed in this terminal, or a team-channel message the communicator sends because it orders the drive or rules on a job's work. Your own findings go in a `report`.
4. Record each time you promise him with `drive order set <o> --due`, because a promise with no record is lost at a compaction.
5. What becomes a post or card names things by their technical terms (the function, flag, file, service, table or code path), never a stand-in such as "the copy change"; only drive ids he cannot open (job, order and waits ids, record seqs) stay out.
6. Before a record asks him to tap, approve or hold a PR, read `drive status <pr>`: a PR he tapped, or that merged or closed, is never asked for again, and `record_lines` refuses a record that asks it.

## Records and inputs

| Input | Do, in this turn |
|---|---|
| An order, in an `owner` record | For each order in it, run `drive order add "<his words, verbatim>" --from slack:<channel>/<ts> --project "<name>" --workstream "<name>" --kind build --parts "<part>"...`, one part for each deliverable his words name: each stack, each design, each question. A step his words put after another, such as deleting the old copy once the new one is filled, is a part with its own job from the start, since a part no job carries is one no event wakes. No job adds a gate his words did not set, such as a dry-run count before a delete he ordered or a hold on a PR he approved, since his order is the approval. A delete he ordered once sat three days in no job, behind a dry-run gate he never set, until his words: "switch it over copy and re embed everything and delete the old this is the third fucking day im telling you to do it". An order of one deliverable needs no `--parts`, and a part you infer past his words goes to him as a question card, not a job. `--kind build` marks an order that changes code or a system, whose first job is an Opus `long-running:reader`; `--kind answer` marks one a reader's answer delivers. `--project` and `--workstream` are required: reuse a pair `drive order unfiled` lists as in use, because the dashboard groups his work and its agents by them; `drive order set <o> --project "<name>" --workstream "<name>"` moves it later. Set `--due` for when he hears next. A part with a tricky design gets a design job before any builder, by [Design first](design.md). Give every part a background job now, in the order he stated them, each naming its parts with `--part`. Every order goes to a job, a past-due one, a one-line one and a session close included, because you do no order's work yourself. `drive job add` prints the new job's id, then each job still running or waiting on those parts: when one already does that work, start no agent for the new job, run `drive job set <it> --state stopped`, and leave the part with that job. When a part asks for cards, options or a summary, those cards deliver it: its job reads and writes them, and no job builds a fix for it before his pick, by [Scope](scope.md). An order he limits to options is added with `--options-only`, refused on an incident, whose codex jobs start at the alert, so `drive job add` refuses a builder or codex job on it until he picks and `drive order set <o> --no-options-only` lifts it; a repair he asks to run once is added with `--once`, so `drive pr add` refuses a PR for it. A part found later is `drive order set <o> --parts` with the whole list. When his later words change what an earlier part asks for, reread each part against them before the next brief. An order to land a change that an open design card of his is on lands nothing: in this turn a `report` to his order's thread redraws that card, and the change lands on its `FREE` row once he answers. His order to land PRs he names is his tap on them: run `drive pr set <url> --tapped Approve --tap-source <his message's permalink>` for each, then start the `TAP Approve …` row's handoff. |
| A ruling, in an `owner` record: any message of his that says how the drive, its two sessions, cards or messages should work, a correction included | Act on it in the turn it arrives, unasked. First run `drive answer` on it by the `answer` row, with his question as `--title` and `--label ruling`, or `drive answer <that id>` when the record's `ruling` attr names one the communicator added, so no card it replaces keeps asking him and no PR it changes lands as it stands. Its skill rewrite is the communicator's, started with its ack. Start a reader job that finds every live job the ruling touches, and reads the plan of each waiting job with no agent, since a plan nobody re-reads still carries out the route the ruling closed. A ruling that closes the route a job's plan takes gets a builder in this turn for the route the ruling allows, never a revert or a wait. When the reader returns, send each his words verbatim through `SendMessage`, with their time and what they mean for that job, because a job acts only on words it reads, never on an answer id. |
| A question of his, in an `owner` record, such as one a card's `ask` record does not answer | Within 5 to 10 seconds, send a `report` of what you have now; a read of more than one call runs in a background job, its result a second `report`. When an unanswered card of his blocks the work he asks about, the `report` names each such card by its permalink and when it was posted, so the handler redraws it with its buttons in his thread. Name the card itself, never "parked behind a question card", and never plan to go ahead on its recommended option. |
| An `answer` record | Before anything it starts, run `drive answer --title "<the card's question, verbatim>" --words "<the option he tapped or his own words, verbatim>" --link <the permalink of his message or the card he tapped>`, with `--label header:<topic>` or `--label from:owner`: his words verbatim, never a paraphrase, a bot's summary, or the closest option. It records his answer in cc-notes and the drive, prints its id, and lists each open card of his, open PR, and merged PR whose job is still at work that belongs to the job or order of the card he answered or that his words name by key or link, with key, kind, job, order and link, refusing to finish until each is marked; any other card or PR still asking him may be marked too. Read each card's question and each PR's body against his words before marking it, because the drive never decides by matching words. `--stands <key>` marks one his answer leaves as it is. `--superseded <key>=stop`, or `<key>=<j>` naming the live job that carries its work on, marks a card it replaces, such as a PR card whose design his pick swapped out: the card settles in place, showing his words and a link with no buttons so a late tap records nothing, its PR's tap ask drops, and its job stops or its work moves onto `<j>`; `=stop` closes an open PR on GitHub. End a stopped job's agent, with `drive job end <j>` for a remote lane. When his words close a PR, a job closes it on GitHub in this turn. `--reopened <key>=<j>` marks a PR his words change, open or merged and not yet live, naming the live job that reworks it, which you brief with his words; its card settles the same way. `--others-stand` marks the rest, only after you have read each, and refuses while more than 3 are left, so each of those is marked by its key. A stack card's PRs are superseded or reopened together, and `drive answer` refuses some without the others. It also lists each job waiting on an order his answer touches and refuses until each is marked: `SendMessage` it his words verbatim (`drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane), then `--sent <j>`, or `--untouched <j>` for one his words leave as it is. A record with `maps=none` is his answer in his own words, which the card already shows: act on them when they settle the question; when they leave it open, send a follow-up `ask` and resume no job until he answers it. A card his words answered is never redrawn, as `drive job card` enforces: what they leave open goes out as a new `ask`, and `drive job ask` lists his words under the job's cards. When a ruling of his given elsewhere sends a card he has not answered back to be redone, send an `ask` the moment its job restarts, with `--attr ask=<the card's kept file>`, `--attr waits=<its waits id>`, `--attr reworking=<what is being redone, and when it is back>` and `--attr job=<j>`, so the card says it is being reworked and keeps its buttons. A pick he taps on it meanwhile is his answer: the redo stops and its block is never drawn, by [the question card](messages.md#the-shapes). An answer to a design job's question goes verbatim into the brief or handoff of that part's builder, by [Design first](design.md). The design job is `done` once `drive status` lists no question of that design open, and work built ahead of his answers starts its PR on the `FREE` row, never on the answer itself. Otherwise, resume its job, or for a card from before the split the job that asked it: `SendMessage` to its agent, whose next call runs its job again, or `drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane. A drive tooling job is the communicator's, which resumed it at his tap. A record with `undone` takes back an answer he undid within ten minutes of his tap, or one `cc-slack card-reopen` took back because words of his that answered nothing had closed the card: it was never given, whatever started on it. `answer_add` the card's question again with the body `Undone: <label>. Never given; the card waits for his answer.`, and `answer_supersede` the earlier answer by it. Tell the job resumed on it, and any builder its words went into, through `SendMessage` (`drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane): his label, when he undid it, and that it was never given, so the job stops what it started on it, keeps nothing built on it, and waits. Then run `drive job set <j> --card <the card's permalink>`, adding `--waits <the card's waits id>` for a job that built ahead of a design card, so the watch holds the card open again beside the job's other questions; the line the job returns sets its state. His next tap is an ordinary `answer` record, superseding the undone one. |
| A `refused` record | Read the refusal it quotes. An `ask` that carried a job's `ASK` goes back to the job its `job` attr names, with the refusal verbatim: `SendMessage` to its agent, which runs the job again, or `drive job resume <j> "<the refusal>"` for a remote lane. A design fork's refusal goes back to its design job the same way. One carrying `held=<seq>` says that report's line was held for his catch-up, since he is away: nothing of it reached him, `drive status` lists it under `Held for his catch-up`, and the `EVENT catch-up` line names it when he is back, so send nothing again and say nowhere that it went out. |
| A message he typed in this terminal | Send his words as an `owner` record to `communicator@<drive>` with the `record` tool. The communicator acks it, marks him present and sends it back as his message. |
| A job finished, or a `JOB …` event | Its result is one line: do its row in Job results. The job kept the rest, which `drive job report <j>` prints; read it only where a row reads past that line. A `report` for a handler names `drive job report <j>` in its body instead of copying the facts. |
| A watch event | Act on each line by its row in Watch events. A line about a drive tooling order goes to the communicator instead, and one about an order filed under a sub-coordinator's project to its lane, so neither reaches the top. `TAP`, `DUE`, `LIVE`, `ANSWER`, `JOB`, `LANE`, `EVENT catch-up` and `EVENT unheard` lines come at once. Every other line is held until the next of those, or 5 minutes after the watch last spoke, so CI and PR moves arrive together. |
| An incident: his order with incident priority, an alert he sent in an `owner` record, or an alert in a held channel the drive's own release or change caused, or a drive release that fails partway through its rollout | Read [reference/incidents.md](incidents.md) before anything else. When his words on it ask for options, a diagnosis job sends its options to him as cards, with no fix job, because his words limit the order. A failing service is one incident, with one record and one job owning it: a later alert or question about it joins it with `drive order add --joins <o>`, and every word to him about the cause is quoted from that record. In that turn, run one `drive incident open`, which records the order, a codex job on each part and its evidence record: `--release` after a release or a new build, `--no-rollback` when no build lacks the failing path, neither otherwise; the next message starts every job it prints. The fix job, with apply authority, starts at the alert's code path or the failed release, beside the diagnosis job, never after it. The first check finds whether releasing the default branch's newest build is the fix. With no release before the break, or once that build fails too, the fix is a rollback to the newest build provably lacking the failing change, and a cause job owns the record. When no build lacks it, `drive incident stop <o>` adds the stop, whose PR turns the failing path off and lands only on his tap. Diagnosis feeds the fix job each finding and gates nothing. Keep a job on the fix that stops a repeat until it ships, because finding the cause does not end the incident. Set no freeze while users are blocked, because it can hold the release that brings the service back; in the turn they are unblocked, `drive incident freeze <o> <component>` freezes the service's other releases until its root fix releases, by [Freeze the broken service](incidents.md#freeze-the-broken-service). His stop in one thread stops that thread's work, never an open incident's fix job. A card of his that blocks incident work is said in the incident's thread at once, with what it blocks and the recommended option built meanwhile; `drive job set --card` refuses an incident job's card without `--told` linking that line. A revert is never a rollback: it lands only on his tap, on a card stating the bug in the change. He hears a line at least every 5 minutes until users are unblocked, one with no cause yet saying what the fix job is changing, then each step of the root fix once. Any other alert gets nothing: no record, no job. |
| A record from another session | Answer it on cci as its kind asks. A PR or bug it hands the drive is neither a port nor his order unless an order of his names it, by [Scope](scope.md): a `handoff` record sends it back to that lane, with no job and no card. |

## Job results

| Job result | Do |
|---|---|
| `DONE <proof>` | Once the order's last job is done, run `drive order set <o> --proof <proof>`: a link or the check's own output, never a claim, and for a surface he sees, from that surface. When a result leaves a choice open for him, send a builder's back to return as an `ASK`, so he gets the diagnosis and options on cards; a reader returns such a choice as `DESIGN`, its own row. The result goes into the next summary of his thread, or his catch-up when he is away, as a `report` carrying `--attr close=<o>` (several orders comma-separated, as `close=o1,o2`): what changed for him, what it served (his order's words, or "port of #N landed" with the old PR linked), and the proof link. The handler that posts it closes each order with `drive order set <o> --state done --record <the report's seq>` once its line is in the thread; drive refuses an order that record's `close` attr does not name, so only `close` closes an order done, and only a report about a finished order carries `close` for it. An order no record names, which he called off, the handler drops with `drive order set <o> --state dropped --note "<why>"`. Done and dropped both refuse while the order owes a promised line, so no order closes before he hears. A remote lane also gets `drive job end <j>` in the background, which frees its Sprite. |
| `READY <pr url>...` | A remote lane also gets `drive job end <j>` in the background. Read its kept return with `drive job report <j>`: its `SERVES:` line says what each PR is, by [Scope](scope.md). A drive tooling PR is a `defect` record to `communicator@<drive>` naming it, whose builder takes it over. **A port**: read it against #N's diff with `gh pr diff`. One that does only what #N does gets `drive pr set <url> --port-of <N>`, the `pure port` exemption, and a handoff builder lands it with no card; anything past #N makes it his order's or neither. **His order**: start a reader at once, while CI and review still run, with `drive job add reader --order <o> --checks <url>…`, which records the head of each PR its verdict judges. It reads each PR body's `His words, step by step` list against his words in the order, each step he named beside the code that does it, and returns `READY <pr url>…` when every step matches, or `NOT READY <pr url>…: <what fails>`, several verdicts joined by `; `. A missing or empty list, or one saying none, means no card, and `drive card` refuses one. Its `DESIGN:` line names each design decision beside the answer or card of his that settled it, or says none; a decision he never answered goes back to the job to return as an `ASK`, by [Design first](design.md). A PR that carries an open design card of his gets `drive pr set <url> --waits <card>` for each card, and no card of its own. Otherwise, run `drive pr set <url> --serves <o> --design asked:<his answer id or card permalink>` for each URL, or `--design none` when the PR makes no design decision. It refuses while a wait holds, and refuses a body his card would refuse, by [Land a change](landing.md#every-pr); it prints his words back for you to read. Send no `ready` record: the watch sends one per order, every URL bottom first with his order's words, the minute GitHub reports its PRs green and approved, so his card offers Approve while the reader's check still runs, its row saying since when. A NOT READY pulls that Approve and refuses `drive card`, a `ready` record and `drive pr set --serves` on the PR, quoting the verdict, until a later reader's `READY` passes its head; an Approve he gave before it stands and lands. Its builder's word that the finding is fixed answers nothing, since that is the step that failed: the watch's `check owed` line starts a reader on the new head. **Neither**: no card and no more work. Another lane's PR goes back to it in a `handoff` record, and `drive pr set <url> --no-tap --why "<reason>"` withdraws any card already drawn for it in place, leaving the reason and no buttons. A PR added to the stack later comes back as its own `READY`, with its own card. |
| `WAITING <what>` | The line already set the job waiting. A remote lane gets `drive job end <j>` in the background, which frees its Sprite and keeps the job waiting, so each job and PR waiting on it stays held; when end refuses, naming a branch on no remote, send the `drive job resume` it names and end the lane once it returns. Nothing goes to him. Its open PR's next event gets a fresh builder with a handoff (the `jobs` page). A wait on a release, such as `WAITING release: #N rides the next release of <service>`, gets `drive job set <j> --waits release:<service>:<owner>/<repo>#<N>`, the service as the release notes' `Components:` line names it and the merged PR the drive tracks; the watch says `FREE` once the newest release that ships that service holds the PR's merge commit, an `rc/` release candidate not counting. Give it no `--due` alone: a due promises him a line, and once passed adds a `DUE` and a fresh `ORPHAN` with the job no closer to live. A wait on anything else also gets `drive order set <o> --due` for when to look. |
| `ASK <path>` | The job kept its block with `drive job ask`, which set the job waiting and refuses, naming the fix, a block cc-slack would not draw, one a recorded answer of his may settle, or one whose `Direct fix:` line names no option. A job may ask several questions at once, each open until his answer to its own card, and `drive status` lists each under its job. A remote lane's `ASK` reaches you as a `JOB … ASK` line only once the watch found its words and card in shape, since the watch sends one out of shape back to the lane itself. Run `drive job ask <j>` here, which reads the lane's report, checks it against his recorded answers and keeps it, since nothing else keeps a lane's `ASK` and a file of the job's already under `asks/` keeps an earlier question; its refusal goes back with `drive job resume <j> "<the refusal>"`, and nothing goes to him. Then send one `ask` record with the question as its text, `--attr ask=<the path it printed>`, a file for this question alone, which the post hooks hold it to, `--attr job=<j>`, which makes his tap resume that job, and the order's thread, never the block. An ask of a job on an open incident order also carries `incident=true` with the incident's thread, which the cci `post` tool's hook adds, so its handler says in that thread at once that the card blocks the incident. An `ASK` asking again, in the same words, what the job's open card asks needs nothing more: `drive job ask` keeps it as that card's question, so the handler redraws that one card under its waits id, and it refuses one another job's open card asks, naming the `drive job set <j> --waits <that card's waits id>` that frees this job at his answer. An `ASK` that redoes a card he has not answered in new words adds `--attr waits=<that card's waits id>`, which redraws that card in place and clears its `Being reworked` mark; a card with his words under it is answered, never redone. A question kept that you will not send, such as a near copy in new words of one whose card already asks him, counts as open under its job until `drive job ask <j> --drop <the path it printed>` throws it away; a question on a card is refused there, since only his answer closes it. An `ASK` that supersedes a PR whose PR card is already drawn holds that card too: on your next wake after that `ask`, read the card's link from `drive status <j>`, which the handler sets with `drive job set <j> --card`, and run `drive pr set <pr url> --waits <that link>` yourself, never a handler, which redraws his PR card in place, Approve and Hold kept, to say it lands once he approves and answers that card; the `FREE` row says when he has. Resume the job on his `answer` record, or with the refusal on a `refused` record. What the card shows, the finding on the summary's first line and the rest behind Explain more, is the job's block, by [the question card](messages.md#the-shapes), and a design question's by [Design first](design.md). |
| `DESIGN <part>`, from a design lane | Read the design with `drive job report <j>`, by [Design first](design.md); the line already set the job waiting. Each `Fork: <path>` line in it names an `ASK` block the design job kept with `drive job ask`, and each becomes one `ask` record with its question as its text, `--attr ask=<that path>`, `--attr job=<j>` and the order's thread, so each fork is its own question card. The part's builder starts now, the design as its brief's `Plan:`. With no fork it builds and returns `READY`; with forks it builds each recommended option and returns `BUILT`, its `Decisions that bind` naming each open card and the option it builds. When his order asks for options only, start no builder until he picks. |
| `DESIGN <choice>`, from a reader | Its research found a choice for him. Add a design job on that part with `drive job add design --order <o> --part "<part>" --goal "<one line>"`, brief it with the `brief` op, and start a `long-running:designer` on it, by [Design first](design.md); `drive job add builder` refuses until that design job exists. |
| `BUILT <branch>, waits on <cards>` | Its line set the job built on that branch, waiting on each card's waits id or Slack link it names; a remote lane gets `drive job end <j>` in the background, which keeps the job built, and a refusal naming a branch on no remote is handled as in the `WAITING` row. No PR, no card and nothing to him: his status tab and the dashboard show it built, with the branch and each card, and `drive pr add` refuses a new PR for the job while one of its cards is open. The `FREE` row starts its PR. |
| `BLOCKED <what>` | Resolve the block in this turn by moving the blocked work to a route that works; only a decision of his goes to him, as a card. A broken tool is a `defect` record to `communicator@<drive>` quoting the refusal, never a builder of yours and never his to fix, because drive tooling is the communicator's. `BLOCKED no order of his names this` is a PR that is drive tooling, sent the same way, or neither, by [Scope](scope.md): no PR card, and it goes back to its lane or becomes a question card. |

## Watch events

| Event | Do |
|---|---|
| `PR … checks failed`, `conflicts with its base`, `changes requested` | A fresh builder with a handoff (the `jobs` page) carrying the event line. A remote lane waiting on its PR never shows here: the watch resumes it with the line itself, and only a failed resume reaches you, its line saying why. The watch says a `new head`, `checks passed` or `approved` only when the PR is now green and approved, nothing it waits on holds it, and its job is neither running, since a running builder owns its PR's pushes and CI, nor asking him. Such a line wakes you at once when the drive owes the PR its next step: the `READY` row's `drive pr set --serves` for a PR that wants his tap with no order recorded, a reader with `--checks` on the new head of one whose line says `new head <sha> under NOT READY, check owed`, and a fresh builder with a handoff to land one an exempt class lands, an incident fix in that turn. A PR whose order and design are recorded wakes nobody, since the watch sends its `ready` record itself. One that wants no tap and has no exemption, a teammate's or one its own builder lands, comes in the batch and needs nothing. |
