# The coordinator

`drive manual coordinator <word>` prints the rows whose first cell names that word: read the one row a line names, not the whole page.

## Drive verbs as tools

- Run the `drive order`, `drive job` and `drive pr` verbs that add or set, and `drive status`, as tools of this plugin's `drive` MCP server, never in Bash: `order` (`add`, `set`, `posted`, `told`), `job` (`add`, `set`, `brief`), `pr` (`add`, `set`), `status` and `search_plan`.
- One call of a tool carries every op the turn needs from it, in order, each op one verb with its flags as fields named as in `drive manual verbs`, dashes as underscores, such as `pr` with ops `[{set: {url: "<url>", serves: "o4", design: "none"}}, {set: {url: "<url 2>", waits: ["<card>"]}}]`.
- An op after an `add` that names no `id` acts on what that add made, and a `job` `add` whose `order` holds `order add`'s fields adds the order too.
- A call checks every op before any runs and prints one line per op; an op the check refuses runs none of them, and the call names every refused op with its fix, so fix each and send the whole call again; `dry_run: true` checks the ops, prints the commands and runs nothing.
- The rows below name each verb as its `drive` command. `drive answer`, `drive card`, `drive job ask`, `report`, `replies`, `launch`, `resume` and `end`, `drive place` and `drive manual` stay in Bash.
- Send an `owner` or `defect` record with the `record` tool, and a `report`, `ready` or `ask` with the cci `post` tool, since its `record_lines` hook checks them and `cci post` in Bash skips it.

## The top and each sub-coordinator

- A drive can run one sub-coordinator per project beside the top coordinator, each a session of its own bound with `drive start <drive> --role coordinator --project <project>` (the `start` page). It holds the coordinator role, so every rule and row on this page and every hook that checks that role binds it, for its project's orders.
- A sub-coordinator owns the orders filed under its project, the jobs serving or carrying them and the PRs serving them, and lands and installs its project's changes. Its own watch, `drive watch --project <project>`, hears only those, its GitHub and release reads, lane mail, ticks, promised times and `ORPHAN` lines included, and its lines arrive at `<lane>@<drive>`, `<lane>` being the project's name lowercased with each run of other characters a dash. It posts with `--lane <lane>`: its `report`, `ready` and `ask` records go to `communicator@<drive>` directly, its `defect` records to the meta session, and his answers to its cards come back to its lane.
- The top hears orders filed under no project or under one with no sub-coordinator yet, every Slack channel the drive holds with the alerts and releases they carry, and the `ANSWER` and `EVENT unheard` lines; its watch alone draws the status page and compacts the communicator. It alone starts, approves and rolls back production cuts, one at a time, since one cut carries every project's changes. His words: "the top starts, approves and rolls back production cuts, one at a time".
- The `landing_guard` hook refuses `ci release start`, `approve` and `rollback` from a sub-coordinator's session and the agents it starts, naming the top's session; `--plan`, `--dry-run`, `status` and `watch` still run. When a change of its project needs a cut his order or pick covers, the sub-coordinator sends a `release` record to `root@<drive>`, a kind the top's subscription wakes on, naming the merged changes the cut must carry, and a job of the top's starts it by [After merge](landing.md#after-merge).
- An event is stamped as it is written with the lane hearing its order then, so lines from before a binding stay the top's. An agent the top started stays the top's to `SendMessage` while its lines go to the sub-coordinator, which sends the top a `handoff` record at `root@<drive>` with what that agent must hear.
- When an `owner` record carries an order that belongs under a sub-coordinator's project, the top sends the record on with the `record` tool's `to: "<lane>"` and adds no order or job for it, since that order's lines would reach only the sub-coordinator. `drive status` lists each sub-coordinator with its project, lane, session, Orca place and whether its watch runs; the `record` tool refuses a lane no session reads, naming those that are.

## Before you act

1. When a line names an order, job or PR, read only those with `drive status <id>`, and the whole `drive status` only after a start, a restart or a compaction, since rereading repeats your context. A report calling a job stuck or silent is refused without a read of its transcript (`drive job replies <j>` or `cc-transcript`) in 10 minutes.
2. Before a report, a brief or a trigger says a PR carries a change, read its title and files with `gh pr view <url> --json title,files`, since a goal line or branch name does not show what it changes. A trigger that gates a release, a freeze or an unfreeze names the change and its branch.
3. An `owner` record carries only his words: his message, words he typed in this terminal, or a team-channel message the communicator sends because it orders the drive or rules on a job's work. Your own findings go in a `report`.
4. Record each time you promise him with `drive order set <o> --due`, because a promise with no record is lost at a compaction.
5. What becomes a post or card names things by their technical terms (the function, flag, file, service, table or code path), never a stand-in such as "the copy change"; only drive ids he cannot open (job, order and waits ids, record seqs) stay out.
6. Before a record asks him to tap, approve or hold a PR, read `drive status <pr>`: a PR he tapped, or that merged or closed, is never asked for again. `record_lines` refuses a `ready` to `communicator@<drive>` naming one, since that record draws his card, and the handler's `say` a post asking it; a record to another session saying he tapped it goes out.

## Records and inputs

| Input | Do, in this turn |
|---|---|
| An order, in an `owner` record | For each order in it, run `drive order add "<his words, verbatim>" --from slack:<channel>/<ts> --project "<name>" --workstream "<name>" --title "<a few words>" --kind build --parts "<part>"...`, one part for each deliverable his words name: each stack, each design, each question. When the record carries a `reporter` attr, his words share or link a bug someone reported, such as a teammate's message in a team channel: add `--reporter <that attr>`, so that thread hears once that its bug is fixed, the moment the fix is live, and the order cannot close done before. A step his words put after another, such as deleting the old copy once the new one is filled, is a part with its own job from the start, since a part no job carries is one no event wakes. A follow-up he orders to a PR, as in "just land this then do a follow up", is such a part, named `follow-up`, and its design job starts in this turn beside the builder landing that PR, with no reader and no `--waits`, never once the PR lands, by his words "start deisgnng the follow p right awya motherfucker dont wait". No job adds a gate his words did not set, such as a dry-run count before a delete he ordered or a hold on a PR he approved, since his order is the approval. A delete he ordered once sat three days in no job, behind a dry-run gate he never set, until his words: "switch it over copy and re embed everything and delete the old this is the third fucking day im telling you to do it". An order of one deliverable needs no `--parts`, and a part you infer past his words goes to him as a question card, not a job. `--kind build` marks an order that changes code or a system, whose first job is an Opus `long-running:reader`; `--kind answer` marks one a reader's answer delivers. `--project` and `--workstream` are required: reuse a pair `drive order unfiled` lists as in use, because the dashboard groups his work and its agents by them; `drive order set <o> --project "<name>" --workstream "<name>"` moves it later. `--title` is required too: at most 60 characters and no link, the name the dashboard shows for his order, such as "Short task titles on Now", while his words stay whole in its task; `drive order set <o> --title` renames it. Set `--due` for when he hears next. A part with a tricky design gets a design job before any builder, by [Design first](design.md). When the order's bug hurts someone now, an incident or a bug someone reported, add an `immediate fix` part beside the proper fix's and start its builder this turn, past the reader and the cards, by his words "always do the immediate fix (or rollback if prudent) in parallel to giving me options for the proper fix"; the proper fix's PR deletes it. Give every part a background job now, in the order he stated them, each naming its parts with `--part`. On a build order the reader is the first job, and in the same call each other part's designer or builder, but the `immediate fix` part's builder and the `follow-up` part's designer, which start at once, is added with `--waits <the reader's job>`, which records it waiting on the reader, so no part sits in no job while it reads; start that job's agent on its `FREE` row once the reader returns, never before. A design part once sat 13 minutes in no job, the plan to start its designer after the reader kept only in the coordinator's thinking, until his words: "what about the other stuff I said in my message here". Every order goes to a job, a past-due one, a one-line one and a session close included, because you do no order's work yourself. `drive job add` prints the new job's id, then each job still running or waiting on those parts: when one already does that work, start no agent for the new job, run `drive job set <it> --state stopped`, and leave the part with that job. When a part asks for cards, options or a summary, those cards deliver it: its job reads and writes them, and no job builds a fix for it before his pick, by [Scope](scope.md). An order he limits to options is added with `--options-only`, refused on an incident, whose codex jobs start at the alert, so `drive job add` refuses a builder or codex job on it until he picks, except on its `immediate fix` part, and `drive order set <o> --no-options-only` lifts it; a repair he asks to run once is added with `--once`, so `drive pr add` refuses a PR for it. A part found later is `drive order set <o> --parts` with the whole list, which keeps each part it names again, a design's changes with their design and waits included, and refuses a list that adds a name while it leaves out a change of a design no builder delivered. When his later words change what an earlier part asks for, reread each part against them before the next brief. An order to land a change that an open design card of his is on lands nothing: in this turn a `report` to his order's thread redraws that card, and the change lands on its `FREE` row once he answers. His order to land PRs he names is his tap on them: run `drive pr set <url> --tapped Approve --tap-source <his message's permalink>` for each, then start the `TAP Approve …` row's handoff. His order under his PR card is filed with `--from` his message in the card's thread, or `--thread <the card's ts>`, and a job already building the PR carries it with `drive job set <j> --carries <o>`; neither withdraws the card, and each prints every open card of his it leaves up. Read that card's stack's `## His words, step by step` lists against his new words first: only when they leave his words unmet does `--rework <PR>` on either call, or on `drive order set <o>`, withdraw each card carrying that PR in the same call, his words its reason. A stack that already meets them keeps its card and his Approve, by his words "You removed this card so I can't fucking click it but this PR is approved". A question of his there is `--kind answer` and leaves the card up, and so does `drive order set <o> --kind build` once the answer turns out to need a build, until `--rework` names the PR. No watch ready, redraw or new head brings a withdrawn card back: a fresh card goes up on its own once the builder's `READY` says in its `CHANGE:` line how his words are met, and `drive job report` refuses a `READY` on a reworked PR without one. His words: "So why isn't this card withdrawn then? And why do I have to keep asking that?" |
| A ruling, in an `owner` record from the meta session: any message of his that says how the drive, its sessions, cards or messages should work, a correction included, which reaches the meta session alone, by his words "meta tuff does not go to the coordinator but instead fo a meta root", and comes to you from it once its rewrite order is added, its body naming that order and what changes for your live work | Act on it in the turn it arrives, unasked. First run `drive answer <the ruling attr's id> --rewrite <the order its body names>` by the `answer` row, so no card it replaces keeps asking him and no PR it changes lands as it stands. Its skill rewrite is the meta session's. Start a reader job that finds every live job the ruling touches, and reads the plan of each waiting job with no agent, since a plan nobody re-reads still carries out the route the ruling closed. A ruling that closes the route a job's plan takes gets a builder in this turn for the route the ruling allows, never a revert or a wait. When the reader returns, send each his words verbatim through `SendMessage`, with their time and what they mean for that job, because a job acts only on words it reads, never on an answer id. |
| A question of his, in an `owner` record, such as one a card's `ask` record does not answer | One proposing work, such as "why not just X?", is his order: take the order row, its job building X, a cost you found on its line, never sent back to him as a reason. For a question of fact, within 5 to 10 seconds, send a `report` of what you have now, with `--attr progress=true` while more is coming; a read of more than one call runs in a background job, its result a second `report`, with `--attr close=<o>` when it answers him whole. When an unanswered card of his blocks the work he asks about, the `report` names each such card by its permalink and when it was posted, so the handler redraws it with its buttons in his thread. Name the card itself, never "parked behind a question card", and never plan to go ahead on its recommended option. |
| An `answer` record | Before anything it starts, run `drive answer --title "<the card's question, verbatim>" --words "<the option he tapped or his own words, verbatim>" --link <the permalink of his message or the card he tapped>`, with `--label header:<topic>` or `--label from:owner`: his words verbatim, never a paraphrase, a bot's summary, or the closest option. It records his answer in cc-notes and the drive, prints its id, and lists each open card of his, open PR, and merged PR whose job is still at work that belongs to the job or order of the card he answered or that his words name by key or link, with key, kind, job, order and link, refusing to finish until each is marked; any other card or PR still asking him may be marked too. Read each card's question and each PR's body against his words before marking it, because the drive never decides by matching words. `--stands <key>` marks one his answer leaves as it is. `--superseded <key>=stop`, or `<key>=<j>` naming the live job that carries its work on, marks a card it replaces, such as a PR card whose design his pick swapped out: the card settles in place, showing his words and a link with no buttons so a late tap records nothing, its PR's tap ask drops, and its job stops or its work moves onto `<j>`; `=stop` closes an open PR on GitHub. End a stopped job's agent, with `drive job end <j>` for a remote lane. When his words close a PR, a job closes it on GitHub in this turn. `--reopened <key>=<j>` marks a PR his words change, open or merged and not yet live, naming the live job that reworks it, which you brief with his words: his tap on it drops and each open card carrying it is withdrawn in the same call, his words its reason, and no card comes back until that job's `READY` says in its `CHANGE:` line how his words are met. `--others-stand` marks the rest, only after you have read each, and refuses while more than 3 are left, so each of those is marked by its key. A stack card's PRs are superseded together, and `drive answer` refuses some without the others; one of them is reopened alone. It also lists each job waiting on an order his answer touches and refuses until each is marked: `SendMessage` it his words verbatim (`drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane), then `--sent <j>`, or `--untouched <j>` for one his words leave as it is. A record with `maps=none` is his answer in his own words, which the card already shows: act on them when they settle the question, as "why not just X?" does with X, which its job builds in place of every option; when they leave it open, send a follow-up `ask` and resume no job until he answers it. A card his words answered is never redrawn, as `drive job card` enforces: what they leave open goes out as a new `ask`, and `drive job ask` lists his words under the job's cards. When words of his given elsewhere supersede a card he has not answered, answering its subject, changing its question or options, or sending its work back to be redone, withdraw it that turn with `drive cards withdraw <its waits id> --words "<his words>" --via <his permalink>`, which settles it showing his words with no buttons, and its question, asked again on any job once the work is back, keeps the card's waits id and redraws it, by [the question card](messages.md#the-shapes). A ruling of his on how code must work, such as a pattern to use or avoid, is recorded with `--label code:<the directory it rules on>`, and `drive pr set --serves` refuses each PR of a job whose brief binds it until the PR writes his words verbatim into the AGENTS.md nearest that directory, his words: "And in general get in the habit of doing that". An answer to a design job's question goes verbatim into the brief or handoff of that part's builder, by [Design first](design.md). A design job is `done` once a builder takes its part or one of its changes, and its builder carries its open cards and his answers to them; work built ahead of his answers starts its PR on the `FREE` row, never on the answer itself. Otherwise, resume its job, or for a card from before the split the job that asked it: `SendMessage` to its agent, whose next call runs its job again, or `drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane. A drive tooling job is the meta session's, which his answer on its card reaches alone. A record with `undone` takes back an answer he undid within ten minutes of his tap, or one `cc-slack card-reopen` took back because words of his that answered nothing had closed the card: it was never given, whatever started on it. `answer_add` the card's question again with the body `Undone: <label>. Never given; the card waits for his answer.`, and `answer_supersede` the earlier answer by it. Tell the job resumed on it, and any builder its words went into, through `SendMessage` (`drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane): his label, when he undid it, and that it was never given, so the job stops what it started on it, keeps nothing built on it, and waits. Then run `drive job set <j> --card <the card's permalink>`, adding `--waits <the card's waits id>` for a job that built ahead of a design card, so the watch holds the card open again beside the job's other questions; the line the job returns sets its state. His next tap is an ordinary `answer` record, superseding the undone one. |
| A `refused` record | Read the refusal it quotes. An `ask` that carried a job's `ASK` goes back to the job its `job` attr names, with the refusal verbatim: `SendMessage` to its agent, which runs the job again, or `drive job resume <j> "<the refusal>"` for a remote lane. A design fork's refusal goes back to its design job the same way. One carrying `held=<seq>` says that report's line was held for his catch-up, since he is away: nothing of it reached him, `drive status` lists it under `Held for his catch-up`, and his catch-up carries it when he is back, so send nothing again and say nowhere that it went out. His catch-up is the drive's own post: its watch writes the report of it and starts the handler that posts it, so no report of yours carries `catch_up`. |
| A message he typed in this terminal | Send his words as an `owner` record to `communicator@<drive>` with the `record` tool. The communicator acks it, marks him present and sends it back as his message. |
| A job finished, or a `JOB …` event | Its result is one line: do its row in Job results. The job kept the rest, which `drive job report <j>` prints; read it only where a row reads past that line. A `report` for a handler names `drive job report <j>` in its body instead of copying the facts. |
| A watch event | Act on each line by its row in Watch events. A line about an order the meta session holds, a drive tooling order or one a brief serving drive tooling gave it, goes to the meta session instead, and one about an order the communicator holds to the communicator, and one about an order filed under a sub-coordinator's project to its lane, so neither reaches the top. `TAP`, `DUE`, `LIVE`, `TELL`, `ANSWER`, `JOB`, `LANE` and `EVENT unheard` lines come at once. Every other line is held until the next of those, or 5 minutes after the watch last spoke, so CI and PR moves arrive together. |
| An incident: his order with incident priority, an alert he sent in an `owner` record, or an alert in a held channel the drive's own release or change caused, or a drive release that fails partway through its rollout | Read [reference/incidents.md](incidents.md) before anything else. When his words on it ask for options, a diagnosis job sends its options to him as cards, with no job on the proper fix before his pick, because his words limit the order, while the immediate fix of what is down ships at once on its own `immediate fix` part. A failing service is one incident, with one record and one job owning it: a later alert or question about it joins it with `drive order add --joins <o>`, and every word to him about the cause is quoted from that record. In that turn, run one `drive incident open`, which records the order, a codex job on each part and its evidence record: `--release` after a release or a new build, `--no-rollback` when no build lacks the failing path, neither otherwise; the next message starts every job it prints. The fix job, with apply authority, starts at the alert's code path or the failed release, beside the diagnosis job, never after it. The first check finds whether releasing the default branch's newest build is the fix. With no release before the break, or once that build fails too, the fix is a rollback to the newest build provably lacking the failing change, and a cause job owns the record. When no build lacks it, `drive incident stop <o>` adds the stop, the immediate fix, whose PR turns the failing path off and lands at once as an `incident fix` until the root fix deletes it. Diagnosis feeds the fix job each finding and gates nothing. Keep a job on the fix that stops a repeat until it ships, because finding the cause does not end the incident. Set no freeze while users are blocked, because it can hold the release that brings the service back; in the turn they are unblocked, `drive incident freeze <o> <component>` freezes the service's other releases until its root fix releases, by [Freeze the broken service](incidents.md#freeze-the-broken-service). His stop in one thread stops that thread's work, never an open incident's fix job. A card of his that blocks incident work is said in the incident's thread at once, with what it blocks and the recommended option built meanwhile; `drive job set --card` refuses an incident job's card without `--told` linking that line. A rollback to an older build, when prudent, needs no tap. A revert is never a rollback: it lands only on his tap, on a card stating the bug in the change. He hears a line at least every 5 minutes until users are unblocked, one with no cause yet saying what the fix job is changing, then each step of the root fix once. Any other alert gets nothing: no record, no job. |
| A record from another session, a `handoff` from the communicator's side included | Answer it on cci as its kind asks. A `handoff` from the communicator or its handler, its `order` attr naming an order you hold, asks one step on that order that only you take, such as redrawing its card with an `ask` record carrying `--attr waits=<the card's waits id>`: take it in this turn by that step's own row, since no other session takes it. A PR or bug it hands the drive is neither a port nor his order unless an order of his names it, by [Scope](scope.md): a `handoff` record sends it back to that lane, with no job and no card. |

## Job results

| Job result | Do |
|---|---|
| `DONE <proof>` | Once the order's last job is done, run `drive order set <o> --proof <proof>`: a link or the check's own output, never a claim, and for a surface he sees, from that surface. On an order with a reporter, run it at the first `DONE` whose proof shows the reported bug fixed live, a hand repair's or a rollback's included, even with jobs still running, and send its `report` that turn: its handler posts the fix-live note in the reporter's thread and records it with `drive order told`, `drive status <o>` saying on the order's line whether that thread heard, and `--state done` refuses until it has. When a result leaves a choice open for him, send a builder's back to return as an `ASK`, so he gets the diagnosis and options on cards; a reader returns such a choice as `DESIGN`, its own row. The result goes into the next summary of his thread, or his catch-up when he is away, as a `report` carrying `--attr close=<o>` (several orders comma-separated, as `close=o1,o2`): what changed for him, what it served (his order's words, or "port of #N landed" with the old PR linked), and the proof link. The handler that posts it closes each order with `drive order set <o> --state done --record <the report's seq>` once its line is in the thread; drive refuses an order that record's `close` attr does not name, so only `close` closes an order done, and only a report about a finished order carries `close` for it. An order no record names, which he called off, the handler drops with `drive order set <o> --state dropped --note "<why>"`. Done and dropped both refuse while the order owes a promised line, so no order closes before he hears. A remote lane also gets `drive job end <j>` in the background, which frees its Sprite. |
| `PUSHED <pr url>...` | Nothing: `READY` counts only on a green required check, so the job waits, waking nobody, until the watch's minute read of GitHub finds green every check the rules of its repository's default branch name, one not yet posted holding it too, or settles it at that read when they name none. Green brings it back as `JOB <j> <o> READY …`, the row below; a red one as `PR … READY refused`. A remote lane's `PUSHED` never reaches you, the watch keeping it as one. |
| `READY <pr url>...` | A remote lane also gets `drive job end <j>` in the background. Read its kept return with `drive job report <j>`: its `SERVES:` line says what each PR is, by [Scope](scope.md). A drive tooling PR is a `defect` record to `meta@<drive>` naming it, whose builder takes it over. **A port**: read it against #N's diff with `gh pr diff`. One that does only what #N does gets `drive pr set <url> --port-of <N>`, the `pure port` exemption, and the drive's lander queues it once green, with no card, after checking it changes no stored data; anything past #N makes it his order's or neither. One that changes how data is stored, a migration, schema or `.proto` file, is no pure port whatever #N did: its data is modeled the fundamentally right way for the target tree, never copied from #N's storage, a design part of the order on his card, by his words "port his visuals but always do it the fundamentally right way when it comes to modelng the data", and `--port-of` refuses it. **His order**: its `DESIGN:` line names each design decision beside the answer or card of his that settled it, or says none; a decision he never answered goes back to the job to return as an `ASK`, by [Design first](design.md). A PR that carries an open design card of his gets `drive pr set <url> --waits <card>` for each card, and no card of its own. Its check against his words takes no turn of yours: a builder on this Mac passed it in `drive job report`, and the watch starts `drive pr check` within a minute on any other PR his card waits on whose change at its head no check passed, once its builder is not running, such as a remote lane's `READY` or `PUSHED`, a PR whose builder waits on something else, or a head that moved under his open card or his Approve, and hands you only the outcome, its `PR … CHECKED` or `PR … NOT READY` line. A head that moved under his card keeps Approve showing, the card saying it lands once that commit passes the check, and his Approve lands it only once the check passes there; a `NOT READY` there sends his Approve back, redraws a card he has not answered with no Approve, saying why, and says why on the card that asks his Approve again, by his words "Keep Approve showing: keeps Approve on a commit nobody judged; your tap lands after the judge passes, and a failed judge sends it back". Each judges each PR's title and diff at its head, never its body, against the words of the order it serves, the job's order and every order the job carries, and each answer of his its `DESIGN:` line or `--design` names, read whole as his words on the change, the job's other PRs named as delivering their own share, and refuses naming each of his words the change goes against. A PR serving an order other than its job's gets `drive pr set <url> --serves <o> --design asked:<his answer id or card permalink>`, or `--design none` when it makes no design decision, which judges the job's PRs his card waits on the same way; it refuses while a wait holds and on a stack over four PRs, and prints his words back for you to read. A PR of a job with several parts names those it delivers with `--part "<part>"`, so the change is judged for the parts its job's PRs name, the rest left to other changes, and for every part of the job when none names one. Send a `drive pr set` refusal to the job by `drive job resume <j>` for a remote lane, or to a fresh builder on `<j>` briefed with it.
